# The Value Equation

**Figure p55 is the single most important image in the book. Send it when explaining this.**

```
        Dream Outcome  x  Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
Value = -----------------------------------------------------
           Time Delay  x  Effort & Sacrifice
```

**Highlighted (p55):** the two on top you seek to increase; the two on the bottom you seek to decrease.

The four, as the prospect's own questions (p56):
| Driver | The question in their head | Goal |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | "What will I make?" | Increase |
| Perceived Likelihood of Achievement | "How will I know it's going to happen?" | Increase |
| Time Delay | "How long will it take?" | Decrease |
| Effort & Sacrifice | "What is expected of me?" | Decrease |

## The most important strategic point in the chapter (p56)
Beginners work the **top** of the equation: bigger claims, bigger promises, more social proof. It is easy and it is lazy, and anyone can make a promise, so it is the least differentiating.

**The best companies in the world attack the bottom.** Apple made the iPhone effortless. Amazon made buying one click and delivery near-immediate. Netflix made TV instant. Immediate, seamless, effortless.

**Get the bottom to zero.** It is a division, not an addition, deliberately: if the bottom approaches zero, value approaches infinity. "If you can make the bottom part of the equation equal to zero, you're golden."

## Driver 1: Dream Outcome (p59-60)
- It is **the gap between their current reality and their dreams** (p59, highlighted).
- Sell the vacation, not the plane flight.
- People want: to be perceived as beautiful, respected, powerful, loved; to increase their status. **Status is usually the real driver.** (p59)
- Frame benefits in terms of **status gained in the eyes of others** (Pro Tip, p60).
- **Highlighted (p60):** "money drives status for men more than being handsome does."

## Driver 2: Perceived Likelihood of Achievement (p61)
- People pay for **certainty**. "How likely do I believe it is that I will achieve the result I am looking for if I make this purchase?" (p61, highlighted)
- The plastic surgeon example: would you pay more to be the 10,000th patient than the first? **"If you're a normal, sane person, a lot more."** (p61, highlighted) The surgery is identical. Only the track record changed.
- Increase it with: proof, track record, guarantees, testimonials, showing the work.

## Driver 3: Time Delay (p61-63)
- Two elements: the **long-term outcome** and the **short-term experience** along the way.
- Build in fast wins. **"People who experience a victory early on are more likely to continue with something than those who do not."** (p63, highlighted)
- Pro Tip, Fast Beats Free (p63): the only thing that beats "free" is "fast." MVD vs DMV line-skipping, FedEx vs USPS, Spotify vs free music, Uber vs walking. **If you compete against free, double down on speed.**
- Liposuction vs 12-24 months of dieting: people pay $25,000 for the afternoon version and $100/mo for the slow one.

## Driver 4: Effort & Sacrifice (p63-65, figure p64)
- The ancillary costs, tangible and intangible: the things they must give up, learn, endure.
- Done-for-you beats do-it-yourself precisely because the effort is removed.
- Fitness vs liposuction effort table (p64): waking up early, meal prep, constant hunger, soreness, risk of injury, new clothing, embarrassment vs "full sleep" and "be sore for two to four weeks."
- **Decreasing effort and sacrifice, or even just the *perceived* effort and sacrifice, massively boosts the appeal of your offer.**

## The scoring exercise (p65-66, figure p66)
Rate any offer 0 or 1 on each of the four drivers. Meditation vs Xanax for "relaxation, decreased anxiety, feelings of well-being":

| Driver | Meditation | Xanax |
|---|---|---|
| Dream Outcome | 1/1 | 1/1 |
| Perceived Likelihood | 0/1 | 1/1 |
| Time Delay | .5/1 | 1/1 |
| Effort & Sacrifice | 0/1 | 1/1 |
| **Overall** | **1.5/4 (Low)** | **4/4 (High)** |

That is why Xanax is a multi-billion dollar product and multi-level meditation businesses are not. Not a claim that Xanax is better. A claim about perceived value.

## How to use this on a real offer
1. Score the offer 0-1 on each driver, honestly.
2. Find the weakest one. That is where the work is.
3. If the weak one is on the bottom (time or effort), that is the highest-leverage fix and the most defensible.
4. Ask: what would take this to zero?
