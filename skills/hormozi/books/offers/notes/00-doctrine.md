# Core doctrine

## The one line the whole book hangs off
"Make people an offer so good they would feel stupid saying no." (TJ, p13, **highlighted**)

## Grow or die
There is no maintenance. The market grows ~9%/yr, so standing still is falling behind. In a fast-growing market you may need 20-30%/yr just to keep pace. (p22, **highlighted**: "if your company isn't growing, it's dying")

## The only three ways to grow
1. Get more customers
2. Increase their average purchase value
3. Get them to buy more times

Collapsed further: get more customers, and increase each customer's value. Nothing else. (p22-23)

Cap math: `10 new clients/mo x $1,000 lifetime value = $10,000/mo ceiling`. To break the ceiling you move one of the three numbers.

## Commodity vs Grand Slam
- **Commoditized** = price-driven purchase = race to the bottom.
- **Differentiated** = value-driven purchase = category of one, no comparison.

**Highlighted (p25):** "if a prospect compares your product to another and thinks 'these are pretty much the same, I'll buy the cheaper one,' then they commoditized you."

### The definition to quote (p25, highlighted)
> "It's an offer you present to the marketplace that cannot be compared to any other product or service available, combining an attractive promotion, an unmatchable value proposition, a premium price, and an unbeatable guarantee with a money model (payment terms) that allows you to get paid to get new customers, forever removing the cash constraint of business growth."

A Grand Slam Offer makes the purchasing decision **"between your product and nothing"** (p25, highlighted), which means you can **"sell at whatever price you get the prospect to perceive"** (p25, highlighted).

## What it actually does, mechanically
1. Increased response rates (more clicks)
2. Increased conversion (more sales)
3. Premium prices (more per sale)

Same ad spend, same eyeballs, three multipliers stacked.

## The money math, real numbers (p27-29, figures p27 + p29)
| | Commodity | Grand Slam |
|---|---|---|
| Ad spend | $10,000 | $10,000 |
| Impressions | 300,000 | 300,000 |
| Response rate | 0.00013 | 0.00033 (2.5x) |
| Appts booked | 40 | 100 |
| Close rate | 16% | 37% (2.3x) |
| Appts closed | 5 | 28 |
| Price | $1,000 | $3,997 (4x) |
| **Cash collected** | **$5,000** | **$112,000 (22.4x)** |
| ROAS | 0.5:1 | 11.2:1 |

2.5 x 2.5 x 4 = 22.4x. The fulfillment work is identical in both. Only the offer changed.

## The two problems every entrepreneur has (p16)
1. Not enough clients
2. Not enough cash

Both are downstream of the offer.

## Key business terms as Hormozi defines them (p23-24)
- **Gross profit** = revenue minus the direct cost of servicing one ADDITIONAL customer. ($10 lotion costing $2 = $8, 80%.)
- **Lifetime value (LTGP)** = gross profit over the customer's whole lifetime. $1,000/mo x 90% margin x 5 months = $4,500. Indirect costs (admin, software, rent) are NOT in it.
