# Payment plans and trials

The two downsells that **keep the price the same and change only when the money arrives.** Use these before you ever consider changing what they get ([03-downsells.md](03-downsells.md)).

## The insight the whole chapter rests on (p107)
> "a huge percentage of the time 'it costs too much' really means 'this costs too much up front.'"

> "people think discounts work because people pay less for the product. But when you peel it back a layer, it's really because they pay less in the moment. So Payment Plans get the best of both worlds. They get more buyers because customers pay less in the moment. But they also boost your profits because customers still pay full price over time."

**And the honest warning (p106):** payment plans are a gamble.
> "They make you more money when you get more customers and those customers complete their payments. They make you less money when people cancel before you turn a profit. You lose the most when people who would have paid in full take a payment plan - and cancel early."

## The seven-step Payment Plan Downsell (p107-109, figure p105)
Work down in order. **Stop the moment they buy.**

**1. Reward for paying in full rather than punish for paying over time.**
Normal businesses charge interest: "It's $10 if you get it right now, but it's $15 if you pay over time." He inverts it: present the price *with the interest already included*, then offer prepayment as a discount.
> "Instead, I say 'It's $15... but it's $10 if you prepay it. You save $5... that's what most people do.'" (p107)
Same math, and it gives you a price anchor.

**2. Offer 3rd-party financing, then credit card, then layaway.** (p108)
- **3rd-party financing:** another company pays you now, the customer pays them later. Takes work to set up, worth it.
- **Credit card:** "would you rather I decide your payment terms or you decide?" They pick, you tell them to use a card, you get paid today. "It's wild to me that this reframe works. But, it does."
- **Layaway:** they pay in installments and only get the product once paid in full. Most flexible for them, lowest risk for you.

**3. Half now, half later.** (p108)
Ask "When's the next time you get paid?" then "Wanna just put half down today and the rest down when you get paid?" If not: "What's the most you can put down today?" then "Great. We'll put that down today and put the rest when you get paid. Fair enough?"
> "I like scheduling payments off of paychecks since most people get paid every two weeks. This boosts 30-day profit way more than monthly payments."

**4. Check they still want the thing.** (p108)
> "No payment plan will satisfy a customer who doesn't want the thing."

"On a scale from 1-10 how bad do you wanna do this?" **8 or above:** keep offering payment plans. **7 or below:** ask "Why not a 10?" then move to a Feature Downsell.

**5. Split into three payments.** A third now and a third on each of the next two paychecks, or the next two months. (p108)

**6. Evenly spread payments** across the whole service. Gym Launch was sixteen weeks, so he charged sixteen weekly payments. (p109)

**7. Offer a Free Trial** in exchange for a card - the Trial With Penalty, below. (p109)

> "This Payment Plan Downsell process makes up to nine offers. And if you think that sounds crazy, you're probably making way less money and serving way fewer customers than you could." (p109)

### The simpler version: "Seesaw" downselling (p109)
For fewer steps or less experienced salespeople. Ask "Would you rather have giant monthly payments or tiny ones?" They say tiny. Then: "It normally costs XXX. And if you prepay it today, you get a huge discount and zero monthly payments. That work?"

If they can't: "If you can't afford it up front, I totally get it. We'll simply adjust the down payment until you get a monthly rate you like." Bigger down payments still get rewarded with lower monthlies.

### The rules around it
- **Payment plans have built-in upsells.** Periodically re-offer the original paid-in-full discount during the plan. "Customers forget they have the option. So when we give it to them, some jump at the opportunity." Pay your salespeople the same bonus to chase it. (p109)
- **Align payment dates with paycheck dates**, and if a card declines, run it a few more times the same day. "I often recoup a third of my declined payments by adding this little process." (p109-110)
- **The test that proves your payment plan is working (p110):** close rate should go up while **the number of paid-in-fulls stays the same.** If paid-in-fulls fall, you've moved people who would have paid in full onto plans. Worked example: 10 leads, 3 sell at full price; with a downsell you sell 3 more, for 6 total. You keep the up-front cash from the first three.
- **Start high and work down**, because billing cadence changes retention. Profitwell churn data across **14,000 businesses** (p110):

| Billing | Monthly cancellation rate |
|---|---|
| Monthly (12x/yr) | **10.7%** |
| Quarterly (4x/yr) | **5%** |
| Annual (1x/yr) | **2%** |

## Trial With Penalty (p113-121, figures p113, p116, p117, p118)

**The structure:** they get it free **so long as they meet your terms.** If they don't meet them, they pay.

The clean contrast with Win Your Money Back (p114):
- **Win Your Money Back:** they pay, and get it back if they meet the terms.
- **Trial With Penalty:** they pay nothing, and only pay if they *don't* meet the terms.

> "So Trial With Penalty isn't 'here's my thing - see if you like it.' It's 'here's my thing, you get it for free so long as you do this stuff... which makes you a perfect fit for my next offer. And if you don't, then you have to pay for it.'" (p114)

**Use the same criteria as your Win Your Money Back offer.** "You can swipe these directly from Win Your Money Back - Attraction Offer #1." (p116) At the end of the trial they have done the things that make great long-term customers.

**The arithmetic (p114):** you close 3 of 10 on the Attraction Offer, downsell another 4 onto a trial, then upsell 3 of those after. **Three sales become six.**

### The five steps (figures p116, p117, p118)
**1. Offer the trial last.** Only after they've clearly refused your first offer. (p116)

**2. Always get a credit card.** (p117)
> "Record their info, hold onto the ID, and motion for their credit card saying. 'What card do you wanna use?'"
If they balk: "That's just how we've always done it." If they still refuse, show them out.
> "If someone doesn't agree to put their card down and do the work, I won't sell them. They complain more and they convert less. Not worth the hassle." (p117, Pro Tip)

**3. Set the expectation of staying and paying.** (p117) Ask directly: "If this program got you the result, will you stay long-term?" If they say no, there is no point giving them a trial. Full script in [09-scripts.md](09-scripts.md).

**4. Explain the fees - AFTER getting the card.** (p118)
> "If you explain the fees before you get the card, you will get more resistance. So explain after with a 'this is just how we've always done it' attitude."
He has customers initial separately next to the fee clauses, to force the salespeople to explain them.

**5. Make check-ins required**, because check-ins are the upsell slots. "Obviously we charge if you miss these because it's the only way we can get you results." (p118)

### How to charge the penalty (p116)
$500 product, ten things to do: he'd rather bill **$50 per mess-up than one $500 fee on the first mess-up.** Unless missing once genuinely wrecks their result, in which case make the fee reflect that. Both work.

### Upselling out of a trial - three cases (p119)
1. **They like it:** billing is already set up. Meet anyway and offer a longer term or higher value version.
2. **They hate it:** ask what they'd have liked different, agree with them, be angry at yourself, never blame them, then offer the higher level thing that actually fits. **"I can get about half of these people to buy."**
3. **They didn't use it:** reach out repeatedly first. Offer to waive the fee if they meet with you. "I don't like billing non-starters. A small fee isn't worth a 1-star review."

### Tuning it (p119)
- Nobody takes the trial → **lower the requirements or penalties**
- They take it but don't follow through → **emphasise how the fees help them, and make sales meetings mandatory**
- They don't stay afterward → **emphasise staying and paying, deliver better, and make sure the back end matches the front end**

### The rest
- **Just call it a Free Trial.** "Otherwise, people may get scared and confused. No one wants to be penalized." (p119)
- **Let people make up for goofs.** Getting billed discourages people; an offer to make it up converts. But if they miss that too, you're justified in billing. (p119)
- **Which downsell for which business (p119-120):** Pay Less Now or Pay More Later for **physical products and one-time services.** Trial With Penalty for **recurring products and services** - and only where the customer has to do work to get results.
- **A $1 first month beats a free one when you need the card to work.** "The small price means the card will probably work when the automatic payments start." (p120)
