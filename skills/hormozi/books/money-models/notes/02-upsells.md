# Upsells

**The job:** maximise 30-day profit from customers you already have.

> "When an offer solves a problem, another appears. You upsell the solution to the problem your offer reveals. So, every offer opens the door to an upsell...even upsells! Often, upsells make the majority of the profit. They make or break a Money Model." (p68)

## The burger math - why the first offer usually isn't the money (p68, figure p68)
A burger shop makes $0.25 profit on a $2.00 burger. On that alone it needs ~10,000 burgers a day to barely survive.
- Add fries: +$0.75
- Make it a meal (drink): +$1.75, so profit goes $0.25 to $2.00, **an 8x increase**
- Supersize for a buck more: profit goes to $3.00, **an 11.6x increase**

> "your first offer doesn't always make the profit. In other words, the thing you sell the most isn't always the thing you make the most profit on." (p69)

> "If you want to win, you have to figure out your version of 'Do you want fries with that?' If you don't, others will." (p69)

## Why upsells fail (p69)
- You offer something they don't want (too different, or doesn't solve their problem)
- You offer it at the wrong time (before they've experienced the problem)
- You offer it the wrong way (they don't believe you)
- Or a combination

## The three shapes an upsell can take (p69)
- **More** of what they just got (quantity)
- **Better** versions of it (quality)
- **New or complementary** stuff (different)

## 1. The Classic Upsell (p71-76, figures p71, p75)
**"You can't have X without Y."** Solve the customer's next problem the moment they become aware of it.

> "your core offer solves one problem and creates another. Your upsell immediately solves that next problem." (p72)

Car: can't have it without insurance, without gas, without a late checkout. Bike: helmet, lights, puncture-resistant tires. Course on exercising: "You can't out-exercise a bad diet."

**The rules that matter:**
- **Actually do it.** "You'd amazed how many businesses come to me and only sell one thing. I usually just tell them 'You barely have a business - you have a front end.'" Months later they report 5x'ing the business. (p73)
- **Offer the more profitable upsell first.** (p73)
- **Get them to say no to say yes.** The fur coat dealer's line: "You don't want to store anything else do you?" People are trained to answer no, and no here means yes to what you just offered. (p72-73)
- **Surprise and delight.** If you hold four bonuses for fence-sitters and they say yes early, give all four anyway. Surprises them, and everyone ends up with the same thing so nobody feels cheated later. (p73)
- **The hyper buying cycle.** "Most buyers enter a 'hyper buying' cycle when they decide to do something new." Weddings, new hobbies, babies, moving. If you serve those moments, keep making offers. (p73-74)
- **Use free bonuses to create the problem your upsell solves.** The free earmuffs cost materials, but customers paid $30 to store something they got for free. (p74)
- **Speed raises take rates.** "A $10,000 thing you get later is worth less than a $10,000 thing you get now." Get it into their hands before they've said yes if you can - "It's way harder to give something back than it is to say no." (p74)
- **Bundle and name it.** "It's easier to sell someone one thing than nine things." Name packages after the customer type or result: "Fastest results" bundle, "Transformation package," "Minimum package." Then you can peel items out as downsells. (p74)
- **Integrate the upsell into the first thing they buy.** His meal plans included optional supplement suggestions, so people asked about supplements. (p74)
- **BAMFAM - Book A Meeting From A Meeting.** End every appointment by scheduling the next. Quoting Sharran: "A customer should know the next time they see you - and why - before they leave." (p74)
- **Charge for guarantees, warranties and insurance** instead of giving them away. An art studio used to replace damaged portraits free; he had them ask for 10% extra, and **30% of customers now buy what was previously free.** Add 5-50% onto price. (p76)

### The Magnetic Middle - engineering which size they pick (p75, figure p75)
Three options, and the middle price decides the winner.
- Small $5, **Medium $8** (rather than the rational $7), Large $9 → **more people take large**
- **Small $6** (rather than the rational $5), Medium $7, Large $9 → **more people take medium**

> "Bottom Line: If you have a lot of customers buying small, you can bump them to mediums. If you have a lot of customers buying the medium, bump them to large. If you have a lot of customers buying large, raise all your prices." (p75)

## 2. Menu Upsell (p77-85, figures p77, p84)
**"You don't need that... you need this."** Four tactics in order:

1. **Unsell** what they don't need. Cross items out. "instead of asking if they want to buy it or not, you explain what they don't need as a way to get them excited about what they do." (p80) He kept products in stock purely to cross them off.
2. **Prescribe** what they do need. Two components: explain how it integrates with what they already bought, and personalise exactly how to use it. "instead of asking if they want to buy it or not, you explain how to use it as if they already have." (p81) He wrote the dosing instructions straight onto the order form and stopped asking.
3. **A/B** - ask their preference, not their permission. "Instead of asking if customers wanted to buy a product, yes or no, we ask which product they prefer: A or B. Either choice results in an upsell." (p81)
4. **Card on file.** "Do you want to use the card on file?" - "instead of asking if they want to pay or not, you refer to ways they already have." (p81)

> "This took me ten years to learn. May you get the same value in ten minutes." (p81)

**Anything can be made A/B (p83):** quantity, start dates, payment method, flavour, time slot, media format, delivery speed, size, colour, material, which staff member, call or text.

**Always add a nudge** if the customer is inexperienced: "This is my favorite," "X is usually a safe bet," "Tuesday's sessions are a little smaller if you like that." Nudge harder on whatever you want to move. (p83)

**Sold out? Take payment and delay delivery.** Lets you sell selection you don't carry. (p83)

**Let employees "game the system" on purpose** - they enjoy it, they have inside knowledge, and it converts. (p83)

### The Economist Play - the decoy that sells the bundle (p84, figure p84)
The Economist offered digital $59 and digital+print $125. Print sales collapsed. They added a decoy:
- A: Digital $59
- B: **Print only $125**
- C: Digital + Print $125

Customers took C.
> "Present three options. Option A, Option B, and Option C (Both)... but you make the price of (C) the same as the more expensive option (B)." (p84)

## 3. Anchor Upsell (p87-91, figure p87)
**Offer the expensive thing first.** If they gasp, rescue them with a cheaper but acceptable alternative.

The five steps (p89):
1. Present the anchor - the really expensive thing
2. Get "The Gasp" - expect them to freak out about the cost
3. Come to the rescue - ask if they care about *what makes it premium*
4. Present your main offer - expect relief and a much better-looking deal
5. Ask how they want to pay - which card do you prefer?

**Numbers and rules:**
- **Make the premium 5-10x the price of the main offer.** (p91)
- The suit story: $16,000 anchor, then a $2,200 suit against a $500 budget, plus $300 of accessories. "after seeing the $16,000 price tag on the first suit, everything seemed cheap." (p88)
- **The main and premium offers must share the same primary features.** Change only the secondary ones. "Most people just want a suit. A few people want a fancy suit. A suit is the primary feature." (p91)
- **Do not treat the anchor as fake.** "If You Treat The Anchor Like A Fake, So Will The Customer." You must actually sell it and they must actually consider it. (p90)
- **Make a premium offer you'd be happy to deliver.** A friend invented one he didn't want them to buy and it failed; they rebuilt it into something real and **tripled his profits.** (p90)
- **Some people buy the anchor.** "Expensive premium offers add outsized profits with fewer sales." (p91)
- Anchored customers spend a bit more than they planned to. (p91)

**The Pro Tip worth memorising (p89):**
> "The Only Thing Worse Than Making A $1,000 Offer To A Person With A $100 Budget...is Making A $100 Offer To Someone With A $1,000 Budget."

In the first case you lose $100. In the second you lose $900.

## 4. Rollover Upsell (p93-98, figures p93, p97)
**Credit some or all of what they already paid toward the next offer.** The one he calls the missing link in his own Money Model.

Three decisions: **who** to upsell, **what** to upsell, **how** to roll the credit. (p94)

**Who (p94-95):**
1. Old customers you want to re-engage (6+ months since last purchase)
2. Your own upset customers, as a better alternative to a refund
3. **Other people's** upset customers
4. Regular current customers

**How to price it (p97):** make the next offer **at least 4x the rollover credit.** Applying the whole first purchase then discounts by 25% at most. You do not have to credit the full amount - test the sweet spot.

**The winback number (p96):** he recorded personalised videos for 200 past customers offering $4,000 of credit to return. **About 20% took it. One day of recording produced roughly $1,900,000 of annual revenue.**

**Stealing competitors' upset customers (p95-96):** scrape contact details off negative product reviews, then roll over whatever they still owe their current provider as credit toward a longer agreement with you. The script is in [09-scripts.md](09-scripts.md).

**Do rollovers before refunding.** "If you did a bad job (hey, it happens), roll over for a 'do over.'" (p96)

**Add urgency - make it one time only.** "if they want the credit, they've gotta take it now. If not, no big deal. They can still pay full price later." (p96)

### The Gift Card Play (p97, figure p97)
Sell $200 gift cards for $20 (90%+ off). **Limit two per customer. They can only be used on other people.** Buyers give them as gifts, so you get introductions. When the recipient arrives, roll the card over.
- **Rule: make the gift card value 20% of the price of what you sell next.** $200 card → an offer with at least a $1,000 price tag.
> "People pay you to refer their friends." (p97)
