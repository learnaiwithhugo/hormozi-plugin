# Continuity

**The job:** one last sale that keeps paying. "Continuity Offers are awesome because you sell once, but get paid again and again." (p133)

## The trade, with his numbers (p133)
Offer a $1,000 thing to 100 people, 10 buy → **$10,000 now, $0 later.**
Offer the same thing at $50/month, 40 buy, they stay 20 months → **$2,000 now, $40,000 later.**

And the second-order effect: 40 customers to upsell instead of 10. "A massive difference." (p134)

## The rule that decides where continuity goes in the sequence
> "This illustrates the pros and cons of continuity. You can attract more customers compared to something more expensive, but you make way less money now. That makes it tough to use as an Attraction Offer on its own." (p134)

So **continuity goes last, not first.**
> "I use Continuity Offers differently. I make them last. I start with profitable Attraction Offers. Then make my Upsell and Downsell Offers. Then, I offer Continuity. And if they accept, I upsell a bulk amount of time or product at a discount." (p157)

**Author Note (p139) - the one that kills the standalone-membership idea:**
> "No successful continuity businesses I've seen has a standalone membership offer. They all have other bells and whistles to upsell. Main reason: Continuity Offers are tough to advertise profitably. Nobody wants to make a recurring commitment to something they haven't tried."

**When continuity is the wrong shape (p134):** "It's silly for someone to pay for a one-day workshop...forever." That's a payment plan. But a single price for a service delivered forever is a mistake too. **If your customers get ongoing value, it probably makes sense for them to make ongoing payments.**

---

## The three Continuity Offers
1. **Continuity Bonus Offers** - add good stuff if they join
2. **Continuity Discount Offers** - take away cost if they join
3. **Waived Fee Offer** - a fee they avoid by committing

He prefers rewards over punishment, and two of the three use rewards. "But, there will always be times when a more traditional contract makes sense. In those situations, I like Waived Fee Offers." (p157)

## 1. Continuity Bonus Offers (p137-143, figures p137, p142)
**Give them something great if they sign up today. The bonus is usually worth more than the first payment.**

**The gym story numbers (p138).** Before: 34 of 100 signed up for the six-week challenge, half (17) converted to membership later. After: only 15 took the challenge but **40 went straight into continuity**, and **8 of those 40 took a six-month prepayment upsell.** Membership sales tripled and up-front cash went up.

**The rules:**
- **Focus on the bonus, not the membership.** "'Join my membership program' isn't nearly as compelling as 'get this free valuable thing.' So advertise that." (p140)
- **Keep bonuses related to the core offer** or you attract the wrong customers. "don't advertise a free t-shirt to upsell tech services." (p140)
- **Make bonuses things you already have and do.** Past newsletters cost nothing extra. Onboarding has to happen anyway - "you might as well slap a price on it and give to 'em as a bonus. If you value it, they will too." (p140)
- **Cross the physical/digital line.** Physical bonuses on digital memberships, digital bonuses (live stream classes) on a physical gym membership. "This strategy often lowers the cost of getting a customer more than the cost of the bonus. And that's the point." (p140)
- **Use realistic bonus pricing.** Made-up values don't anchor and cost you trust. Best source: things you have actually sold before at real prices. (p141)
- **Titles are free bonuses.** Silver, gold, diamond, double diamond after 3, 6, 12 months. A friend found her customers cared more about the title than any other bonus, and introduced themselves by it. (p141)
- **Anchor the bonus, then reveal the route to free.** Sell the bonus's benefits, let the price shock them, then: "Do you want to know how you can get this for free?" (p141)
- **Stack them.** "on top of that... when you become a member you'll get... amazing thing 1, amazing thing 2, amazing thing 3." Mention individual dollar values. (p141)
- **Want commitments? Trade for them.** Only give the bonus to people who commit 3-6-12+ months. "This nets fewer sales, but more committed customers. This is the trade you make." (p143)

### The continuity vs one-time pricing curve (p142, figure p142)
His tested data on how much bigger the standalone price has to be to push people into continuity, against a $199/mo membership:

| To get this % choosing continuity | Make the standalone | Example |
|---|---|---|
| 50% | 1.33x more | $399 standalone ($266/mo) |
| 60% | 1.66x more | $499 standalone ($333/mo) |
| 70% | 2x more | $599 standalone ($399/mo) |
| 80% | 2.33x more | $699 standalone ($466/mo) |
| 90% | 2.66x more | $799 standalone ($532/mo) |

> "The exact numbers matter less than the principle. The smaller the standalone price compared to the continuity price, the more people buy the standalone. The larger the standalone price compared to the continuity price, the more people choose continuity." (p142)

**The read-across (p143):** "people pay 33% more to avoid continuity. In other words, even if you charge 33% more for a one-time purchase, half will buy it!"

**And the bulk prepay lever (p143):** "Let's say you offer 'buy five months get one free.' Only one out of every eight people has to take the upsell to raise 30-day profits by 50%!"

## 2. Continuity Discount Offers (p145-151, figures p145, p147, p148)
**Give time away free if they commit to buying over time.** The trash-hauler in the story fronted a whole free year for a five-year paid contract, and sold the business for a fortune.

### The four ways to apply the discount (p147-148)
| Way | How it works | Best for |
|---|---|---|
| **1. Up front** | free time first, official term starts after | industries that reliably enforce contracts - cell phones, storage, real estate, equipment, anything with collateral. **Skip it if you have high churn.** Gets customers but delays cash |
| **2. At the end** | they earn the bonus time by making every payment on time | lowers churn, converts fewer |
| **3. Spread over time** | $600 discount over 12 months = $50/mo off. Can be kept for life if all payments land on time | keeps cash flowing while giving the full discount |
| **4. After the first 1-2 payments** | "first and last month," "last month up front," or an activation fee, then the discount | collects cash to cover advertising, and proves the payment method works |

Stated as a summary rule (p151): **frontloaded discounts convert more customers but may churn more; backloaded discounts convert fewer but churn less; spreading it keeps cash flowing while giving the full discount.**

**Extend the term, don't eat into it (p149).** Three months free on a one-year signup should mean **15 total months, not 12.** "I prefer to start with extending the term. Then, I can Feature Downsell a shorter one."

**Gift cards as the delivery mechanism (p149).** Give the discounted time as a physical gift card, usable after the first three payments, and transferable to a friend. "And now you've got a lead magnet! Beyond that, many people simply forget to use it. In that instance, you just got a full-priced sign-up."

**Lifetime discount at your churn point (p150).** Advertise a lifetime lower rate they earn by staying past X - and set **X to the month your average customer drops off.** The rice company he saw offered one-time price, 5% off subscription, or **15% off if you stayed subscribed five straight months.** "I'm sure they figured out that it was just beyond where most people canceled."

## 3. Waived Fee Offer (p153-155, figure p153)
**Structure:** join month-to-month and pay a startup fee, or commit to a year and the fee is waived. Cancel inside the term and the fee comes back.

Worked example (p154): 12-month commitment, $1,000/mo, $5,000 fee.
- **Option A:** $5,000 one-time plus $1,000 for month one, then $1,000/mo, cancel whenever.
- **Option B:** fee waived for a 12-month commitment at $1,000/mo. Only pay the $5,000 if you break it early.

**The numbers:** make the fee **3-5x your monthly rate.** Commitment length **at minimum a year.** (p155-156)

> "Bottom Line: Customers will stay longer if leaving costs more than staying." (p154)

**Why it works twice (p155):**
- **Fees get them to start** - committing has immediate value because it avoids a fee.
- **Fees get them to stick** - "if the cost to quit exceeds the cost to stay, they will probably stay."

**How to justify the fee (p155):** "It costs us money to get you started. If you only wanna test us out, you cover those costs. If you commit longer, I'll cover them."

**The dials:**
- **Bigger fee** → more people take the commitment. **Smaller fee (1.5-3x monthly)** → more people go month-to-month and you get more up-front cash. (p155)
- **Drop the fee once they complete the commitment.** "It doesn't stick forever. This makes it equitable." (p155)
- **If more than 5% want to cancel early, look into it.** "Pricing incentivizes sticking but it can't (and shouldn't) overcome a terrible product. You want to nudge them, not handcuff people into paying for something they hate." (p155)
- Best for **one year and longer**, and for services that take time to work - SEO, investing, weight loss. "It keeps people committed when they get emotional." (p155)
- The spicy version (p155): donate the cancellation fee to a cause they hate. "What cause do you absolutely hate?... Great. If you cancel early, I will be donating your setup fee to them."
