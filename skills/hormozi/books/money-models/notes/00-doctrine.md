# Doctrine

**Read this first when the question is vague.** It is the frame the rest of the book hangs on.

## The one-line definition
> "A Money Model is a deliberate sequence of offers. It's what you offer, when you offer, and how you offer it to make as much money as you can as fast as you can." (p161)

Not one offer. A **sequence**. (figure p11)

> "Making one offer works better than making none. And making more offers works better than making one. Combining offers in a sequence makes a Money Model." (p15)

## The three definitions, in ascending order (p169)
1. **A Money Model** is a series of offers designed to increase how many customers you get, how much they pay, and how fast they pay it.
2. **A good Money Model** makes more profit from a customer than it costs to get and service them **in the first 30 days**. That is the bare minimum.
3. **A $100M Money Model** makes more profit from *one* customer than it costs to get and service *many* customers in the first 30 days - "which removes cash as a limiter to scaling your business."

## The 30-day rule, and why it is 30 days
> "I like to cover my costs of getting a customer within 30 days. Main reason: any business can get interest-free money for 30 days in the form of a credit card. If you clear your balance before the end of the month, it works just like normal money. So you can use credit to get a customer, pay it back, and then use it again to get the next customer." (p13, Author Note)

That is the whole reason the number is 30 and not 90. Thirty days is the length of a free loan anyone can get.

## The four offer types (p15, figure p15)
| # | Type | Job |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **Attraction Offers** | turn strangers into customers |
| 2 | **Upsell Offers** | get people to spend more cash |
| 3 | **Downsell Offers** | get people to say yes when they would have said no |
| 4 | **Continuity Offers** | keep people buying |

The order is not decorative. Attraction first because "if you're not getting customers, you need one of those first." Then upsells, then downsells, then continuity on top. (p16)

## The three stages, and why you build them one at a time (p161, figure p164)
- **Stage I: Get Cash** - Attraction Offers get more customers for less
- **Stage II: Get More Cash** - Upsell and Downsell Offers make more money from them faster
- **Stage III: Get The Most Cash** - Continuity Offers maximize their total money spent

> "if you try to start a bootstrapped business, from zero, on your own, with a 'finished' Money Model... it will collapse on top of you." (p161)

His own build order, verbatim (p161):
> "First, I get customers reliably then I make sure they pay for themselves reliably then I make sure they pay for other customers reliably then I start maximizing each customer's long-term value then I spend as many advertising dollars as I can to print as much money as possible."

**"Perfect One Offer At A Time. It's tempting to implement a whole Money Model at once. Don't."** (p165)

## The compounding math that makes this worth doing
> "But what if you make your customers twice as valuable, you get twice as many of them, and get those customers at twice the speed?... your business grows 8x faster. And if you triple them...your business grows 27x faster." (p13)

Three levers, multiplied: **value per customer x number of customers x speed of payment.**

## Why bad Money Models kill businesses (p12)
The death spiral, in his order: spend money to get customers, realise at month end you spent more than you made, cut back on advertising, get fewer customers than you can handle because you can't afford them, cut advertising altogether, float the business on personal cash and loans and credit, sell percentages of the business to keep the lights on, wait months or years to make the money back, fall further behind, lose it all.

> "In traditional business, the slow drip of profits from lots of customers eventually pays for a single customer. This drip starves the business of cash." (p12)

The trap stated cleanly: a business that makes $500 profit on a $100 customer is a great business **only if you already have cash in the bank.** If it takes two years to get the money back, you run out first. Two options:
- **Option 1:** wait two years to get paid and pray you don't run out of money.
- **Option 2:** get paid fast and grow as much as you darn well please.
> "A good Money Model is option 2." (p13)

**Author Note, p165:** "Bootstrapped Businesses Must Get Customers At A Profit. Unless you get outside investors...start with a fortune...or, have an endless source of free customers...achieving a Money Model is the only way you can profitably scale."

## The seven ground rules he states before any tactic (p16-17)
1. **All businesses have Money Models. It makes a business a business.** Switch the poor person mantra "this won't work for my business" to the rich person mantra "how will I make this work for my business?"
2. **Some Money Models work better in some businesses than others.** Copying what "they" do will disappoint you. Design your own.
3. **If a customer asks for their money back, give it back.** "Avoid the headache. And if you made a goof - fix the goof."
4. **Hard selling is for weak products.** "If someone doesn't want something, that's OK. Don't convince someone against their will."
5. **Obey the law.** Advertising law, especially around "free," changes and tightens. Check with lawyers.
6. **Be transparent.** "State the facts. And if the facts aren't compelling, change reality to make them compelling or learn to frame them in a way that is. Don't lie."
7. **Any offer can be used on its own, at any time, in any order.**

## Where this book sits against the other two (p169)
- **$100M Offers** answered *what should I sell?* An offer so good people feel stupid saying no.
- **$100M Leads** answered *how do I find these people?* You advertise.
- **$100M Money Models** answers *how do I get them to buy it?* A Money Model.
