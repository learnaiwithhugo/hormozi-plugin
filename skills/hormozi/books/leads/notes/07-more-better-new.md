# More, Better, New

The framework for *I'm doing the Core Four and still not getting enough leads.*

**The story that opens it (p159-160).** A business owner says he's saturated the chiropractor niche. Alex asks: revenue? $2M/year. Ad spend? $30k/month on Facebook. Click-to-close conversion? Doesn't know. Other platforms? None. Content? None. Cold outreach? None.

> "And the $30k you spend, on one platform, for a two million dollar business, saturated the $15.1B chiropractor industry? Does that sound reasonable?" (p160)

A second owner in the same niche then says he spent $30k across four platforms **last week.**

Same conversation with a $3M/year weight loss business worried that going past $40,000/month would saturate a platform with **over 1B daily active users**, in a **$60B** industry.

> "There are more leads out there than you can possibly imagine." (p160)

---

## 1) MORE (p161-163, figure p161)
> "Even with no improvements at all, if you double your inputs, you'll get more engaged leads." (p161)

**The Rule of 100:** 100 primary actions every day, for 100 days in a row.

> "I don't make many promises, but this is one. If you do 100 primary actions per day, and you do it for 100 days straight, you will get more engaged leads. Commit to the rule of 100 and you will never go hungry again." (p161)

Applied to each of the Core Four (p162):
| Method | 100 what |
|---|---|
| **Warm reach outs** | 100 reach outs per day (email, text, DM, calls) |
| **Post content** | 100 **minutes** per day making content; release at least one per day per platform |
| **Cold reach outs** | 100 reach outs per day; expect lower response rates, so use automation |
| **Paid ads** | 100 **minutes** per day making paid ads, 100 days straight of running them |

**More ads means better ads means more leads (p162).** Facebook reviewed all advertisers on the platform: **the top 0.1% test eleven times more creative than everyone else.** "Oftentimes, it's not that you can't scale an ad profitably. You just can't scale a *mediocre* ad profitably. And the only way to find the exceptional ads is to make eleven times more of them."

## 2) BETTER (p163-165, figure p163)
> "you do more and more... until it breaks. Then, you make it better." (p163)

**You only get better by testing.** Thousands of tiny tests separate winners from beginners.

**Constraints (p163-164).** Every action a lead takes before becoming a customer is a potential drop-off point. **Test hardest at the step where the most leads drop off.** "Constraints are the points where the smallest improvements create the biggest boost in results."

The arithmetic that proves it (p164) - three steps at 30% optin, **5% apply**, 50% schedule. Improve each by 5 percentage points:
- 30 → 35% optin = **1.16x** leads
- **5 → 10% apply = 2x leads**
- 50 → 55% schedule = **1.1x** leads

**Wildly different results, and the constraint wins outright.**

**The testing cadence (p164-165): one test per week per platform.** Four reasons:
1. Test multiple things at once and you never learn what worked
2. **Steps affect each other** - change step one, more opt in but fewer apply, and you'd never know
3. It forces prioritization - you have infinite possible tests and limited time, so don't waste the week on red vs bright red
4. **You run it long enough to see a real improvement.** Too short, not enough data. Too long, wasted time.

**The Monday ritual (p165):**
1. Look at results, pick winners per platform
2. **Write results into a log of all tests** - "so the next time we do something, we start a zillion improvements later, not at square one"
3. Design the next test to beat the current best. **If you can't beat it in four tries (or one month), move to the next constraint.**

**Front > Back, most times (p165).** The lowest-percentage steps usually sit at the front (1% click, then 30% give contact info). Those are usually the constraint. The call out. The value elements. The offer. The CTA. The landing page headline, subheadline, image.

**Order flip by size (p166).** Under $1,000,000/year in profit → **more first** (you haven't done enough volume for percentage changes to matter). Over $1,000,000 → flip to **"better, more, new."**

## 3) NEW (p166-169, figures p166, p167)

### The Size Of The Pie Fallacy (p166-167)
Most owners look only at the platform and tiny community they market in, see three or four big competitors, and assume those companies split the entire market.

> "A small business uses one of the core four, on one platform, in one specific way, with a very targeted audience... They mistakenly assume the tiny slice of the universe they advertise to is the entire available market!" (p167)

> "This is why most businesses stay small. When they plateau, they think there's no more leads to get... Because, for many, saying 'I'm as big as I can get' is much easier than saying 'I'm not as good at advertising as I thought.'" (p167)

### When to do new (p167)
**When the returns from more-better are lower than what a new placement or new way would give you.** Not before.

### The order (p168)
> New placements → New Platforms → New Core Four

Easy to hard. The rule behind the order: **what gets the most leads for the amount of work.** Nine times out of ten it goes that way.

- **New placements:** Instagram has stories, messenger ads, posts. YouTube has shorts, longs, community posts.
- **New platforms:** Instagram messenger → Facebook messenger. YouTube shorts → Instagram reels.
- **New core four activity:** the biggest jump, saved for last.

## The summary (p168-169)
> "First, you do way more of the advertising that works until it 'breaks.' Then, the next drop off point becomes obvious. Then you keep that level of advertising up while you go back, fix the constraint, and make it better. So really, better and more work with each other more than they work separately."

**The question he asks before investing in a company that needs customers:**
> "What's stopping them from doing ten times what they're currently doing?" (p168)

Sometimes nothing - so do more. Sometimes something needs fixing first. **Answer that and you know what to do next.**

## And the compounding note (p169)
Every combination of the Core Four boosts the others. A business that posts content *and* runs paid ads gets more out of both. A business doing cold outreach *and* content works its warm leads better.

His own record (p169): first business off content + warm outreach · gyms off free content + paid ads · Gym Launch off paid ads + cold outreach · Prestige Labs off affiliates.

> "If you master one, you will be able to feed yourself for the rest of your life. They all work if you do." (p169)
