# The plan

## Open to goal (p254, figure p254)
His three highest-ROI habits:
1. **Waking up early (4-5am)** - "Pro tip, this actually means going to bed early"
2. **Getting right to work** - "No rituals. No routines. I drink coffee and get to work"
3. **No meetings until noon** - no interruptions, fully focused work time

> "I don't think there's any magic to waking up early. But I do think there's magic in a long stretch of uninterrupted work immediately after a long stretch of uninterrupted sleep." (p254)

**"Open to goal"** means you set a daily goal from what you know you can do in a day, and you work until it's hit. Only then do you put out fires, talk to humans, and deal with the rest.

> "Waking up early, getting right to work, and working 8 hours straight has been my highest ROI 'habit stack.' By a longshot." (p254)

He wrote this book inside that block, because it's an asset that brings him more companies. (p255)

> "Whenever I get to a low point where I think 'why do I even bother?' I just try to remind myself 'this is where most people stop, and this is why they don't win.'" (p255)

## The One Page Advertising Checklist (p256, figure p256)

**Step 1: Pick the type of engaged lead to get** - Customers, Affiliates, Employees, or Agencies.

**Step 2: Pick Rule of 100 or Open To Goal.** Commit to your daily advertising actions.

> **Rule of 100: 100/day × 100 days**
> | | Warm | Cold |
> |---|---|---|
> | **Outbound** | 100 warm reach outs | 100 cold reach outs |
> | **Inbound** | 100 minutes of content | 100 dollars of paid ads |
>
> **Bonus: do 100/day of more than one of the Core Four.**

Note the paid ads row: **100 dollars a day**, not 100 minutes, in the checklist version. (p256)

**Step 3: Fill out the Advertising Daily Checklist for that action:**
| | |
|---|---|
| Who | You |
| What | Your offer or lead magnet |
| Where | Platform |
| To whom | Audience / list |
| When | First 8 hours |
| Why | Get X engaged leads or lead getters |
| How | Warm/cold outreach, content, ads |
| How much | 100 or until you hit your goal |
| How many | # of follow ups / times retargeted |
| How long | 100 days or until you hit your goal |

**Step 4: Do this daily action until you have enough money to afford paying someone else to do it.**

**Step 5: When you do, go back to step 1 - make employees your new target lead type.** Repeat steps 1-4 until you have the help you need. Then scale again.

> "You can fill out your one page advertising checklist in about five minutes. And once the naked truth stares back at you, all you have left is to do it." (p257)

**On planning (p257):** "Many skip planning, or worse, they write a one hundred page plan that never gets used. So skip the atrocious waste of time that is writing pages of baloney. Harness the power of laying out your action steps on a single page. **It leaves little room for excuses, distractions, and delusions. You either did the stuff or you didn't.**"

**And the reason the plan is needed at all (p257):**
> "Reading doesn't get people interested in the stuff you sell... advertising does. If you're not telling anyone about the stuff you sell, then you aren't getting anyone interested in the stuff you sell. Period."

## The seven levels (p259-261, figure p259)
Where you are on the advertising totem pole, and what gets you to the next rung.

| Level | Where you are | Primary action |
|---|---|---|
| **1** | Your friends know about the stuff you sell. One offer, one avatar, one platform | **Warm outreach** |
| **2** | You consistently let everyone you know know. You know the exact inputs to get an engaged lead, and consistent customers come from maximizing *your personal work capacity* | **As much warm outreach and as much content as you can consistently do** |
| **3** | You've maxed your personal inputs but not the platform | **Hire people to advertise profitably on your behalf** |
| **4** | Your product is good enough to get consistent referrals. Shoot for **25%+ of customers from referrals** | **Focus on the product until referrals are consistent, then go back to scaling advertising with a bigger team** |
| **5** | You advertise in more places in more ways with more people. New audiences on your best platform → all placements and media types → then another platform, lead-getter, or Core Four activity | **Advertise profitably using at least two methods on multiple platforms** |
| **6** | You hire killers. Executives grow departments specific to a method or platform without you. **Not potential - experienced leaders specializing in exactly what you want** | **Get battle-hardened executives and department heads to take over new channels** |
| **7** | *"I'll come back and edit this chapter once I cross a billion."* | — |

**Level 4 is where most people mess up (p260):** "They let their product slip and never recover."

**Level 2 correction (p259):** "For me, on top of warm reach outs, I maximized my personal work capacity with paid ads, using a case study as my lead magnet. **But looking back, I wish I would have started with posting free content. So I suggest that.**"

**Hire experience, not potential (p261).** He tried cold reach outs twice before it worked the third time. The difference was who ran it: external with experience - failed. Internal with no experience - failed. **Internal with experience - worked.**
> "Pick experience. They should know more than you do. **If you're not learning from them in the interview, you've got the wrong person.**"

**The honest caveat (p261):** "I know this looks clean. But it never is. Real business is messy... you can only find out what works if you try. So you have to try a lot of different things, a lot of different ways, for a long enough time to know for sure."

## What the $100M machine looks like (p262, figure p262)
- Media team scaling tons of free content, all media types, many platforms
- Regular offers to the warm audience for customers or affiliates
- **A ravenous audience that makes anything you launch immediately profitable**
- Teams running and scaling profitable paid ads across multiple platforms
- A cold outreach team getting customers
- An affiliate manager launching and integrating new affiliates
- Recruiters and recruiting agencies bringing in more lead getters
- **A product so good that a third of your customers bring you more customers**
- An executive team driving all of it without you
- More engaged leads than you can possibly handle

**How long:** for business owners who know what to do, **five to ten years.** It took him and Leila more than ten years of their best effort to cross the first $100M in net worth.

> "the bigger your goals, the longer your time horizons need to be. **You want to play games where if you wait, you win.**" (p262)

## The permission-to-fail lesson (p248-249)
At a private ten-person event he'd paid extra to attend, out of his depth and sweating, someone asked the speaker what courses he took. The answer changed his life: **set some percentage of your advertising budget aside to try new things without expecting a return - 1%, 5%, 10% - and consider it an investment in your education.**

> "I felt chills go through me like some judgment demon left my body. He gave me permission to fail. None of this is magic. If he can do this, so can I." (p249)

What followed (p249): tripled the ad budget → $400k June to $780k July → CAC rose → tried new audiences, most failed, one hit → past $1M to $1.2M to $1.5M/month → realized they never followed up with engaged leads → email failed, phone calls failed, **text blasts worked** → $1.8M → tested paid ads harder with more production value → past $2.5M → launched the affiliate program → **+$1.5M/month on top, past $4M.**

> "test until you find something that works. Take massive action. Stay focused. Double down on it until it breaks. Then test until you find the next thing that works, and double down on that." (p249)

> "You either win or you learn." (p249)

2. How to turn leads into engaged leads with an offer or lead magnet, and how to make them

7. **The seven levels** of advertisers and the $100M leads machine in action

## The many sided die (p266-268, figure p266)
The parting fable. You and a friend each get a die - one has 20 sides, one has 200. Only one side is green. **Roll green as many times as you can.**

Rules: you can't see how many sides you have · **roll green and one red side turns green**, and you roll again · roll red, nothing happens, roll again · **the game ends when you stop rolling, and if you stop rolling you lose.**

You roll. At first green comes once in a while. As more red sides turn green, greens happen more. **With enough rolls, hitting green becomes the rule rather than the exception.**

Your friend rolls a few times, hits red, watches you, complains you must have fewer sides, rolls a few more in frustration, hits green, complains it took too long, decides the game is rigged, and quits.

> "once you roll enough times, the die you're given doesn't matter."
> - Die with fewer sides might roll green sooner
> - Die with more sides might roll green later
> - **But a die with a green side always has a chance of rolling green... if you roll it**
> - **Every die hits its green streak when rolled enough times**

> "looking at the other players, you have no idea if it's their 100th roll or their 100,000th." (p267)

The two guarantees (p267):
1. **The more times you roll, the better you get**
2. **If you quit, you lose**

> **"You cannot lose if you do not quit."** (p268)
