# Warm outreach

One-to-one contact with people who already know you. **The cheapest and easiest way to find people interested in what you sell, and most businesses don't do it.** (p52, figure p52)

> "Warm reach outs are a fantastic way to get your 'First Five Clients' for any new product or service. Advanced Folks: Think re-engagement and new product lines." (p53)

It is low-yield per hour and completely reliable. "As certain as the sun rises and sets, it works." (p52)

## The ten steps (p53, figure p53)
1. Get your list
2. Pick a platform
3. Personalize your message
4. Reach out
5. Warm them up
6. Invite their friends
7. Make them the easiest offer in the world
8. Start at the top
9. Start charging
10. Keep your list warm

---

### 1. "I don't have any leads" → everyone has a list (p53-54)
Phone contacts. Every email account you've ever used, and its address book. Every social profile - followers, subscribers, friends, connections. **Add them up and write down the number.** For most people this is their first 1,000 leads. (p54)

### 2. Pick a platform (p54)
The one with the most contacts. That's the only criterion. You'll hit them all eventually.

### 3. Personalize the greeting (p54)
Use something you actually know about them as the real reason to reach out. Check their profile if you don't. **You haven't asked for anything yet - you're checking in and providing value.**
> Ex: "Saw you just had a baby! Congrats! How is the baby doing? How are you?"

### 4. Reach out to 100 people a day (p54)
Up to **three times** - once per day for three days, or until they respond, whichever comes first. Once per week with physical mail.

**Rip off the bandaid (p55):** "The first reach out is always the hardest and takes the longest. Your second reach out will take minutes. Your third, seconds."

### 5. When they reply - the ACA framework (p55, highlighted)
- **Acknowledge** what they said, restated in your own words
- **Compliment** them on it, tied to a positive character trait
- **Ask** another question, steering toward a topic closer to your offer

> Fitness example: "...Wow! Supermom! So hardworking! Managing a full-time career and two kids... do you have time to get workouts in?"

On email and voicemail you do it all at once: 2-3 personalized sentences, then straight to the offer or lead magnet. (p56)

### 6. Make the offer (p56-59, figure p56)
After a normal amount of conversation - 3-4 exchanges by message or phone, 3-4 minutes in person. **Highlighted (p56):** refer to the value equation.

**The Value Equation** (from *$100M Offers*, restated p56-57, figure p56):

> (Dream Outcome × Perceived Likelihood of Achievement) ÷ (Time Delay × Effort and Sacrifice)

Maximize the top two, minimize the bottom two. Show them: you have exactly what they want, they're guaranteed to get it, insanely fast, without lifting a finger or giving up anything they love. Get as close to that as you can **without lying or exaggerating.** (p57)

**The script (p57)** - the whole trick is you are not asking them to buy:
> "...By the way, do you know anybody who is (describe their struggles) looking to (dream outcome) in (time delay)? I'm taking on five case studies for free, because that's all I can handle. I just want to get some testimonials for my service/product. I help them (dream outcome) without (effort and sacrifice). It works. I even guarantee people get (dream outcome) or I work with them until they do. I just had a girl named XXX work with me (dream outcome) even though she (describe the same struggle your contact has)... Does anyone you like come to mind? (Pause) ...and if they say no... Haha, well... does anyone you hate come to mind?"

**This is the highlighted mechanism (p58):** "We're not asking them to buy anything. We're asking if they know anyone." · "Since you didn't ask them to buy anything, you don't come off as pushy." · Some show interest, some refer, some do both - **"in all three outcomes, you win"** and you win **"without pushing anything on anyone."**

The short version when you have no room (p58):
> "I help (ideal customer) get (dream outcome) in (time period) without (effort and sacrifice) and (increase perceived likelihood of achievement)."

**11 ways to raise perceived likelihood of achievement (p59):** your own story · testimonials from people just like them · sheer volume of happy reviews (or, absent reviews, the number of people helped) · certifications and accreditations · numbers, stats, research · experts vouching · a new/unique characteristic they haven't failed with before · celebrity endorsement · a guarantee (skin in the game) · how well you describe their current pain, specifically · demonstrating the outcome live or on a recording.

### 7. Make it easy to say yes - make it free (p60-61, figures p60, p61)
**Highlighted (p60):** "make your offer easy to say yes to" using "the easiest offer enhancer in the world - FREE."

> "Since I'm only taking on five people, I can give you all the attention you need to get brag-worthy results. And I'll give it all for free so long as you promise to: 1) Use it 2) Give me feedback and 3) Leave a killer review if you think it deserves one. Does that sound fair?" (p60, partly highlighted)

**Highlighted (p61): "make the first five free."** Four reasons:
1. You get the reps and calm your nerves - you're just helping, for free
2. You probably suck right now, and people are far more forgiving when they haven't paid
3. You suck less by doing more; free guinea pigs get the kinks out
4. People who get value free are far more likely to review, give feedback, and send friends

**Highlighted (p61):** "free customers can make you money in three other ways: 1) They convert into paying customers. 2) They send you paying customers via referrals. 3) Their testimonials bring in paying customers."

**The Hinge Method for referrals (p62):** don't accept a name and number. Grab the customer's phone, take a photo of the two of you, text it to the referral and to your own number. Virtual: screenshot the video call and do the same. Minimum acceptable: a three-way conversation they initiate.

### What if they say no (p62)
**Highlighted (p62):** "If you struggle to give your stuff away for free, it means either people don't want it (dream outcome), they don't believe you (perceived likelihood of achievement) or the hidden costs (time, effort and sacrifice) are too high." In short: **your free stuff is too expensive.**

So ask why. **Highlighted (p62):** *"What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?"*

> "Yeses give me opportunity. Nos give me feedback. Either way, I win." (p62)

**On being told not to "undervalue yourself" (p63, highlighted):** if anyone tells you that, tell them to hush. **"The objective right now is to learn not to earn."**

### 8. Start at the top (p63-64, figure p63)
Exhaust platform one, move to the platform with the second-most leads, then the third. With 1,000 leads across platforms that's ten solid days of work, a month including follow-ups. By then five or more people will have taken the free offer. (p64)

### 9. Start charging (p64, figure p64)
**Highlighted (p64): "Once people start referring, start charging."** That is the litmus test for "good enough."

Then the ladder: swap "free" in the script for **80% off for the next five**, then 60% off for the next five, then 40%, and so on. **Highlighted:** the "I increase my prices every five" rule also adds urgency, because prices actually go up. Keep raising by 20% every five until you find your sweet spot. (p64)

**Prepay + guarantee (p65, highlighted):** "You can offer a guarantee **only** to people who pay up front." Reason why: people who invest up front are more committed, so you can guarantee their outcome. Dr. Kashey's framing after they agree to buy: *"would you rather pay less today or get all your money back?"*
> **Highlighted:** "Pay Less" = $2000/mo for 3 months = $6000 (no guarantee)
> **Highlighted:** "Get All Your Money Back" = $6000 up front with a guarantee.

Most take the up-front option. If you were going to offer a guarantee anyway, weaponize it to pull cash forward.

### 10. Keep the list warm (p65-66)
Give regular value by email and social. Then Dean Jackson's **nine-word email**:
> "Are you still looking to [4 word desire]?"

No images, no frills, no links. Just the question. Examples: "...buy your dream home?" "...get more sales leads?" "...tone up your arms?" "...start a YouTube channel?" (p66)
> "This message is money for getting leads to engage. And it's among the first things I do when I invest in a new business." (p66)

**The replies are your top-priority warm reach outs.**

## Benchmarks (p67, figure p67)
**Highlighted (p67):** "Warm reach outs should get about one in five contacts to engage. So one hundred warm reach outs should get about twenty replies."

Then: of 20 who reply, ~1 in 5 take the free offer = 4 people. Of those 4, ~1 converts to paid. **≈ 1 customer per 100 warm reach outs.**

**Highlighted (p67):** "with enough volume, you will get a customer."

**The money math to $100k (p67):**
- 500 reach outs/week → 5 customers/week
- $400 product × 5 = $2,000/week
- × 52 weeks = **$104,000/year from warm outreach alone**

| Who | Yourself |

| Where | Phone / email / physical mail / SMS / etc |
| To whom | Your contacts |
| When | First four hours of your day |

| How long | Until you get customers |

## The two limits (p68)
1. **Time.** Four hours a day minimum when starting, first thing in the morning.
2. **The number of people who know you.** You will run out. That's what posting content fixes - it grows the warm audience that warm outreach draws from.

> "You'll learn more in the first ten days of doing 100 reach outs than you did from everything you've ever read or watched." (p68)
