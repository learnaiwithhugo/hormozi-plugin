# The money math

> "efficiency matters more than creativity. All advertising works. The only thing that differs between advertisements is how well they work." (p147)

> "you just need to get it 'good enough' to scale. Why? Because good enough is good enough." (p147)

This chapter answers four questions (p147): how much do I spend · how well am I doing · how do I fix unprofitable ads · what do I wish I'd known.

---

## How much do I spend? Three phases (p147-149)

### Phase One: Track money
Before spending a dollar, set up tracking. **"If you don't track, you're gonna get cleaned out."** Like playing a casino game for as long as you *feel* like rather than as long as you can afford. Get a consultant, watch tutorials, get it set up. End of story. (p148)

### Phase Two: Lose money
> "I've lost money more times than I've made money running paid ads. But every time I make money with paid ads, I make back everything I lost, and then a bunch more." (p148)

**The shape of it:** many small losses, rare huge wins. $100 on ten ads = $1,000. Nine lose it all. One makes $500 back on $100. You're down $500 - most people stop here because they see a loss. **You see a winner.** Then spend $10,000 on the winning ad and make $50,000. (p148)

> "you might lose, nine or ninety-nine times in a row before you win big. But, to win big, you have to see the winners and double, triple, quadruple, 10x down on them." (p148)

**The test budget rule (p148).** Budget **two times the cash you collect from a customer in thirty days** (not LTGP) when testing a new ad. Make $100 in the first thirty days? Let an ad spend to $200 before shutting it off - **as long as it's getting leads.** If it's getting **no leads at all**, kill it before 1x thirty-day cash ($100 in the example).

**Timeframe honesty (p149):** he worked with a business that took **a year** to get paid ads profitable, then made the whole year's wasted spend back in the next month. "It costs money to build an advertising machine... and that's normal. Just make sure you measure the returns over a long time horizon, not next week."

### Phase Three: Print money (p149)
If you make back more than you spend, spend as much as you can.

**Reverse the budget from the sales goal, not the other way round.** Don't ask "How much money should I spend on an ad?" Ask **"How many customers do I want?"** or **"How many customers can I handle?"**

Worked (p149): can handle 100 customers next month, customers cost $100 → $10,000. Ads get less efficient as they scale, so **pad the budget 20%** → $12,000 over thirty days = **$400/day.** Then commit to it.

> "If the number terrifies you, then you're doing it right. Trust the data. This is how you scale. And that's why most people never do." (p149)

## How well am I doing? LTGP:CAC (p149-151, figure p150)

**LTGP = lifetime gross profit** - all the money a customer ever spends minus all the money it takes to deliver it. He uses this instead of "LTV." (p149)

Worked (p149-150): a $15 item costing $5 to deliver = $10 gross profit. Ten purchases over a lifetime = $150 in sales, $50 in cost, **$100 LTGP.**

> "Gross profit is important in general because it's the actual money you use to acquire customers, pay rent, cover payroll, and... everything else to run your business." (p150)

**The rule (p150):**
| | |
|---|---|
| LTGP > CAC | profitable advertising |
| LTGP < CAC | losing money |
| **LTGP / CAC > 3** | the threshold |

> "Every business I invest in that struggles to scale has at least one thing in common - their LTGP to CAC ratio was less than 3 to 1. As soon as I get it above 3 to 1 (either through decreasing CAC or increasing LTGP), they take off. **This is a pattern I personally observed, not a rule.**" (p150)

**Two levers (p150):** lower CAC (better ads) or raise LTGP (better business model). *"For maximum money... I prefer to do both."*

### The diagnosis that matters most (p151)
> "They often think they have crappy ads (high CAC) when, in reality, they have a crappy business model (Low LTGP)."

> "The cost to acquire customers, between competitors in the same industry, is much closer than you'd think. **The difference between the winners and the losers is how much they make off each customer.**"

**How to tell which one is broken:** research your **industry average CAC.**
- **CAC below 3x industry average (good)** → your ads are fine, work on the business model (LTGP)
- **CAC above 3x industry average (bad)** → work on your advertising (CAC)

**And the ceiling on cost-cutting (p151):** "lowering the cost of getting a customer by $100 will eventually take more work than making an extra $100 from them... Costs can only approach zero but how much you make can go up to infinity." Chasing efficiency past that point is like trying to save your way to a billion dollars.

## My ads aren't profitable - Client Financed Acquisition (p151-154, figures p152, p153)

**The problem:** LTGP is bigger than CAC, but not after the *first* purchase. The profit from purchase one is often less than the cost to get them, and the full LTGP takes months to collect. **That cash flow problem is what actually stops you scaling.** (p151)

**The rule:**
> "if your customer spends more than it costs you to get and fulfill them - in the first 30 days - then you have the funds to scale now and forever. I call this client financed acquisition." (p151)

**Why thirty days:** any business can get interest-free money for thirty days on a credit card. Cover the cost to get and fulfil inside that window, square the balance, and **money is no longer your bottleneck.** (p152)

### Worked example (p152-154)
Starting position:
- $15/month membership, $5 to deliver = **$10 gross profit/month**
- average member stays 10 months = **$100 LTGP**
- CAC = $30 → **3.3:1 LTGP:CAC.** The ads make money.

**But (figure p153, "OLD WAY"):** you spent $30 and get $10 back per month. Ten bucks trickles in until you break even **two months later.** You should 100% make that trade - but you now have a cash flow problem.

**The fix - immediately sell them more (figure p153, "NEW WAY"):**
- Offer a **$100 upsell at 100% margin** that **1 in 5** new customers take
- $100 ÷ 5 customers = **$20 average upsell per customer**
- First purchase $10 + upsell $20 = **$30 gross profit inside the 30-day window**
- CAC $30 - $30 collected = **free customers**

Every $10/month after that is gravy. Pay off the card, do it again.

> "It's how I've scaled every company I've started for the past seven years past $1M/mo in the first twelve months - without outside funding." (p154)

**Bottom line (p154):** "Figure out a way to get your customers to pay you back in the first thirty days so you can recycle your cash to get more customers."

## Three lessons (p154-155)
1. **Don't confuse sales problems with advertising problems.** A company spent twelve weeks and $150,000 on ads. Right leads on the phone, not buying. The owner said advertising didn't work - **the ads were fine, the sales sucked.** He gave up "six inches from gold," at an estimated **-$30M in enterprise value.** The test: *if your engaged leads have the problem you solve and the money to spend, and they're not buying, your ads work fine - you have a sales problem.*
2. **Your best free content makes your best paid ads.** "If you make a free content piece that generates sales, or performs very well, nine times out of ten it'll make a great paid ad." Including **UGC** - customer testimonials and reviews that perform as free content often make killer ads, at no extra work.
3. **If you say you suck at something, you will probably suck at it.** He said "I'm not techy" for four years. Then hated his website designer more than he hated tech: *"If this idiot can do it, so can I."* **Four years of lost money reversed with four hours of concentrated effort.**

## Your first ad (p155)
Search "HOW TO PLACE A [PLATFORM] AD." Place one for $100. **"Don't go all the way to the end then chicken out. Spend the gosh darn money. Rip off the bandaid. As soon as you do - you're no longer an observer, you're in the game."**

Start with an acceptable amount you're willing to lose each month. Expect to lose it. **"You won't be earning, you'll be learning."**
