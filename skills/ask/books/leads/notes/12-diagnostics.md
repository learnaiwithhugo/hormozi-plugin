# Diagnostics

**Start here when the question is vague.** Work down the list; the first "no" is usually the answer.

---

## 0. Is it an advertising problem at all?
**The single question (p208):**
> **"Do my engaged leads have the problem I solve and the money to spend?"**

| Answer | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| **No** - they aren't qualified | **Advertising problem.** Wrong people are seeing it |
| **Yes**, they're buying but there aren't enough | **Advertising problem.** Not enough volume |
| **Yes**, they're qualified and not buying | **Sales problem.** Your ads work fine |

> A company spent twelve weeks and $150,000 on ads. Right leads on the phone, not buying. The owner concluded advertising didn't work and quit "six inches from gold" - an estimated **-$30M in enterprise value.** The ads were great. The sales sucked. (p154)

Don't fire your sales guy for an advertising problem, or your advertising people for a sales problem. (p209)

---

## 1. "I'm not getting enough leads"
> "if you aren't getting as many leads as you want, you're not doing the core four with enough skill or with enough volume." (p48)

Two questions, in this order:
- **Are you doing 100 primary actions a day?** (p161) If not, that's the answer. More comes before better for anyone under $1M/year in profit. (p166)
- **Are you doing more than one of the Core Four?** Almost everyone stuck is doing one, on one platform, in one way. → `07-more-better-new.md`, the Size Of The Pie Fallacy (p167, figure p167)

**The saturation claim is almost always false (p160).** $30k/month on one platform against a $15.1B industry is not saturation.

## 2. "I'm counting leads and they don't convert"
You're probably counting **leads**, not **engaged leads**. A lead is anyone you can contact. An engaged lead is someone who has shown interest in what you sell. **"Engaged leads are the true output of advertising."** (p24) → `00-doctrine.md`

## 3. "My ads aren't profitable"
Three checks, in order (`06-ad-money-math.md`):
1. **Is LTGP:CAC above 3?** Below 3 is the common factor in every business he's seen struggle to scale. (p150)
2. **Is it the ads or the business model?** Look up your **industry average CAC.** Below 3x average → your ads are fine, fix the business model (LTGP). Above 3x average → fix the ads. (p151)
3. **Do you break even inside thirty days?** If not, you have a cash flow problem, not an ad problem. Fix with **client financed acquisition** - an immediate upsell that covers CAC plus fulfilment inside the 30-day window. (p151-154, figure p153)

**And check you didn't kill a winner too early.** Test budget is **2× the cash a customer generates in 30 days** - unless it's producing no leads at all, in which case kill before 1×. (p148)

## 4. "Nobody takes my free thing"
**Highlighted (p62):** "If you struggle to give your stuff away for free, it means either people don't want it (dream outcome), they don't believe you (perceived likelihood of achievement) or the hidden costs (time, effort and sacrifice) are too high." **Your free stuff is too expensive.**

Then ask them why. **Highlighted:** *"What would I have to do to make it worth it for you to continue?"*

Also check:
- **Is the lead magnet actually good?** "make your lead magnets as good as your paid stuff" (p40, highlighted). Sucky fluff makes people who might've become customers decide you suck - and tell others.
- **Have you tested the name?** Headline first, then image, then subheadline. Improving them can 2x-10x engagement. (p36)
- **Is it easy to consume?** Multiple formats gets 2-4x take rates for the same work. (p39-40)

**On cold audiences specifically:** the game-planning-session lead magnet failed for four months. Swapping it for real free service **3x'd take rates.** (p114) The standard is **"stuff they actually pay for,"** not "so good they should pay for it." (p115)

## 5. "My content isn't growing an audience"
**The scoreboard is not your opinion (p84, highlighted):** "your audience grows. If it's not growing, your stuff isn't that good."

Diagnose by the three jobs of the content unit (p76):
- **Hook** - are you picking topics from your own experience, using at least two of the seven headline components, and matching the platform's format? (p77-81)
- **Retain** - are you embedding unresolved questions with lists, steps or stories? (p82-83)
- **Reward** - do you actually satisfy the promise the hook made? Think **value per second.** (p84)

Then the two most common structural mistakes:
- **You're asking too often.** The give:ask minimum is ~3.5:1 and that's what *mature* platforms do. To grow, over-give. A friend's podcast **shrank** after he started asking too frequently. (p88-91)
- **You're making it for your existing audience, not strangers.** (p86, highlighted)

And the volume answer: *"You just gotta do more bro."* 10x the content produced 10x the growth. (p74)

## 6. "My cold outreach isn't working"
- **Are you tracking the metrics?** Both failures came from hiring people who never tracked well. (p120, highlighted)
- **Benchmarks:** phone ~4 engaged leads per 100 calls; **email, shoot for 3% of the list becoming engaged leads**; DM ~20% reply. (p121)
- **Is the value big and fast enough?** "we're trying to blow their minds in under thirty seconds." (p114)
- **Is it personalized?** One to three pieces of info a friend would know, complimented. (p113)
- **Reading level?** Rewriting below third grade reading level got **50% more responses.** (p114)
- **Follow up count?** Multiple times, multiple ways, then again after **three to six months.** (p118-119)
- **Below 100/day?** "Don't set a daily goal below 100" and "don't stop for 100 days minimum." (p125, highlighted)

## 7. "We get no referrals"
Two causes, and only two (p183):
1. **The product isn't as good as you think.** His question: *"Why are my customers too embarrassed to tell everyone they know about my product?"* If it were exceptional you'd have more business than you could handle.
2. **You never ask.**

**Then measure the equation (p182):** referral % − churn % = growth. If referrals are below churn you're advertising to break even - a hamster wheel. → `08-referrals.md`

**The fastest lever** is usually **Set Better Expectations** - lower your promises until close rates dip, then stop. (p186) It costs nothing and makes the product remarkable by comparison.

## 8. "Growth has plateaued"
Three questions, in order:
1. **What's stopping you from doing ten times what you're doing now?** (p168) That is the exact question he asks before investing.
2. **Where do the most leads drop off?** That's your constraint, and improving it gives wildly higher returns than improving anything else - the 5% → 10% apply step is a 2x, while the same 5 points on a 50% step is a 1.1x. (p164) Front-of-funnel steps are usually the constraint. (p165)
3. **Have you actually exhausted more and better before reaching for new?** New placements → new platforms → new Core Four. (p168)

**And the level check:** if you're at Level 4 - product good enough for consistent referrals - the failure mode is letting the product slip while scaling advertising. "This is where most people mess up." (p260)

## 9. "I don't have time"
That's Level 3 (p260). You've maxed your personal capacity, not the platform. **Hire people to advertise on your behalf**, then train them with document → demonstrate → duplicate. → `09-employees-and-agencies.md`

Or, if you have money and want a *skill* rather than a body: hire an agency **with a six-month deadline and an explicit agreement that they teach you**, then drop to consulting once your team beats theirs. (p215)

## 10. "Which method should I even use?"
> "after I do warm outreach to get my pool of customers going - if I have more time than money, I move to posting content. If I have more money than time, I go with cold outreach or running ads." (p169)

Paid ads last: the skills transfer from the other three, and the other three give you the money. (p156)

**You only need one.** "So, just pick one. Then, max it out." (p169)

---

## The three things that are almost always the real answer
1. **Not enough volume.** 100 a day, 100 days. (p161)
2. **Not enough value given away first.** Free stuff too expensive, lead magnet too weak, asking before you've given. (p40, p62, p88)
3. **Not tracked, so not fixable.** No tracking on ads (p148); no metrics on outreach (p120); no test log (p165); no referral and churn baseline (p197).
