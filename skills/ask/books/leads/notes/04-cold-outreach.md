# Cold outreach

One-to-one, to people who don't know you. **The advanced cousin of warm outreach**, no longer limited by the number of people who know you. (p109, figure p108)

You reach for it when you want to grow faster than you currently are, or when you want **predictability** of lead flow. (p109)

> "It worked 100 years ago. It works today. And when the platforms change, it'll work tomorrow." (p109)

## The one difference: trust
Strangers present three new problems (p109):
1. You don't have a way to contact them
2. Even if you do, they ignore you
3. Even if they give you attention, they're not interested

Solved in that order (p110):
1. Get a way to contact them
2. Figure out what to say
3. Contact them until they're ready and able to listen

## Problem 1: build the list (p111-112, figure p111)
This is the one method where **you pick your targets rather than them picking you.** Only want hedge fund managers over $1B? Golf apparel retailers over $3M? Done. (p111)

Three sources, worked in order of accessibility (**both highlighted, p111**):
- **Step 1 - Software:** "I subscribe to as many softwares as I can that scrape leads from different sources." Search on your criteria, test a representative sample (a few hundred per tool). If contact info is current, leads are responsive and they're who the tool claims - take everything it will give.
- **Step 2 - Brokers:** "I go to multiple list brokers and ask them to make me a list based on my audience criteria." Test sample lists from each; stick with whoever performs.
- **Step 3 - Elbow grease:** join groups and communities holding your audience. Find contact info in the group directory or profiles; prefer contacting outside the group so you don't look like you're only there to mine it.

**The freshness argument (p112):** "If you can search the database, so can everyone else. But if you assemble a list of names yourself, it's less likely that person has already received many cold reach outs from other companies. So they're the freshest." Costs the most time - if you have more time than money, start at step three.

**Interest groups are the warmest cold audience you can get (p112).** Concentrated pools of people already looking for a solution.

**Action (highlighted, p112):** search **"outbound leads scraping tool"** or "database lead scraping" to find tools, and the same way for brokers. **"Put your first 1000 names together."**

## Problem 2: what to say - two factors (p112-115)
**Highlighted (p112):** the "two important factors I emphasize to get strangers to engage: **personalization** and **big fast value**."

### Personalization (p113-114)
Make the cold reach out look like a warm one. **Highlighted (p113):** "Personalization is what gets your foot in the door to get the sale. Basically one to three pieces of information we can find that a friend might know about the prospect. Then we want to complement them on it" - and ideally show how it benefited you.

The phone illustration (p113): an unknown area code gets ignored. But someone who says your name, pauses like a normal person, then says *"I watched a few of your videos and read that recent blog post you wrote on dog training. It was killer! Really helped me out with my doberman... That peanut butter trick really helped"* - you're confused, but you're not hanging up. Opening with "hey man, wanna buy some marketing services?" gets you hung up on.

> "Even if someone doesn't know you, they'll give you more time if you know something about them." (p113)

**Do the research in batches** - yourself, paid help, or software. (p114)

**50% email response bump (p114):** he rewrote the cold outreach template **below a third grade reading level** and got **50% more leads responding.** Run every script through a free reading-level tool.

### Big fast value (p114-115)
**Highlighted (p114):** "strangers give you far less time to prove your worth" and "need a lot more incentive to move towards you." So give away the farm. **Highlighted: "We're not trying to tickle their interest, we're trying to blow their minds in under thirty seconds!"**

**The case that proves it (p114).** The first four months of cold outreach were torture. The lead magnet was a "game planning session." He tested many parts of the process, but **swapping the lead magnet blew everything else out of the water** - from "game planning" (code for sales call) to actually giving as much free service as they could afford. **Take rates 3x'd and cold outreach became a monster channel.**

**Highlighted (p115):** if you forget everything else, "the goal is to demonstrate big value as fast as possible."

The precise standard (p115): *"Give away something for free people would normally pay for."* Note the wording - not "so good they *should* pay for it," but **"stuff they actually pay for."**

**On scripts (p115):** phone and chat scripts are never more than a page or two, cold emails rarely more than half a page. "There are no awards for prettiest script. Get your first 100 conversations or 10,000 emails out of the way before tweaking it."

**Expect multiple steps (p110).** He sells expensive things in multiple steps rather than on first contact. Get interest first, then schedule the sale. Expect **two to three conversations** before a higher-ticket sale. (p118)

## Problem 3: volume (p115-119)
Three levers.

### a) Automate delivery (figures p115, p116)
**Manual** = a person spends time conveying the message each time (live call, personal voice memo, handwritten letter).
**Automated** = record once, send to everyone (pre-recorded voice memo, templated email or text, pre-recorded video).

"you get more engaged leads per unit of time (even if fewer engage by overall percentage)." You have far more people who don't know you than who do, so you don't need to worry about burning the audience. (p115)

### b) Automate distribution (p116-117, figure p116)
Manual: dial each number, click send on each message. Automated: robot dialer, blast 1,000 emails at once.

**The trade rule (highlighted, p117):** "if there are only 1000 hedge fund managers who meet your criteria, you're gonna wanna personalize every one of them." But "if you're targeting women 25-45 trying to lose weight, there are tens of millions of them. So you can get away with less personalization."

**The fewer leads you have, the less automation you should use.** Max personalization with max volume is the deadly combo when tech lets you have both. (p117)

**Allocate 10-20% of effort to brand new untested technology.** If you call five days a week, try a new dialer one of those days and compare. (p117)

### c) Follow up - more times, more ways (p117-119, figure p117)
**Highlighted (p118):** the frame is - imagine you really needed **"to get a hold of your parents because something important came up."** You'd call, text, leave a voicemail. Still nothing? **"You'd call and text them again."**

**Highlighted (p118):** "Contacting someone multiple times multiple ways shows them you are serious." Doing it *quickly* signals importance and raises curiosity.

His sequencing trick (p118): email first, precisely *because* most people don't respond - the non-response is the reason to follow up on another channel. *"Hey I'm calling you to follow up about my email."* You get a response or a real reason to reach out again.

**Highlighted (p118): "Contact each lead multiple times in multiple ways."**

**Then start over (p119).** Three reasons the second pass works: they may not have seen the first series; the moment may have been wrong; **their circumstances may have changed** (the person who fit their skinny jeans before the holidays and doesn't after).

**Highlighted (p119):** "After you've attempted to contact them multiple times, multiple ways, wait three to six months. Then, do it again."

**Don't be a nincompoop (p118):** if someone asks not to be contacted, stop. Not on principle alone - it's more efficient to turn neutrals into yeses than nos into yeses, and a bad reputation follows you.

**New to an outreach team? Shadow the best rep, then double their inputs.** 200 calls becomes 400. "Volume negates luck... Replicate before you iterate." (p119)

## Benchmarks (p120-121)
**Highlighted (p120):** "The two times I failed at cold outreach I hired people who never tracked metrics well." The third person did, and it worked. **Highlighted:** "The person who runs it (maybe you) has to know the metrics of the sales process like the back of their hand."

**Phone (p121):** 100 cold calls/day → 20% pick-up → 25% of those take the lead magnet = **4 engaged leads.** Four hours of calls = 1 engaged lead per hour.

**Email (p121):** 100 personalized emails/day → 30% open → 10% of those reply = **3 engaged leads (3%).** **Highlighted:** "The numbers will vary but shoot for 3% of your list turning into engaged leads." A real portfolio campaign showed 4%: 801 contacted, 428 opened (53%), 48 replied (6.0%), 21 opportunities.

**Direct message (p121):** 100 personal videos or voice memos, name plus one personal line → 20% reply = **20 engaged leads.** Then ACA them into a call.

**The pass/fail line (p121):** you're doing well when the lifetime profit of a customer is **at least 3x** what it costs to get them. "that's the bare minimum" - the portfolio company above gets **over 30:1.**

## The cost model (p122)
A cold-calling team, worked all the way through:
- $15/hour + $50 per shown appointment · $3,600 profit per sale · leads cost $0.10
- 200 leads called per day → ~2 shows per rep per day
- 8-hour day = $120 labour + $100 show commissions + $20 leads = **$240 for 2 shows = $120 per show**
- Close 33% of shows → **$360 to get a client**
- $3,600 profit per client = **10:1 return**

> "That's how cold outreach works. Then, you just add bodies. It is boring and tedious, but brutally effective." (p122)

**Give each rep an explicit number of leads per week (p122).** If someone can work 100 leads at full capacity, give them ~70 - so they have the time and energy to squeeze everything out of them. Same leads for everyone means you can set absolute quotas. *"I give you seventy leads. You give me back seven appointments."* **No leads left behind.**

## Why bother - seven benefits (p123-124)
1. **No content or ad treadmill.** One message, perfected. No ad fatigue or banner blindness, because prospects have never seen anything from you.
2. **Competitors can't see it.** Everything is private. You aren't educating them.
3. **Highlighted: "All you have to do to get more is do more."** It **"becomes like clockwork, bringing a reliable flow of new engaged leads into your world."** You get an **equation: "for every X people contacted, you get Y customers. Then, you simply solve for X."** (all highlighted, p123)
4. **Fewer platform changes.** Rules for cold calling, door knocking and cold email have barely changed in thirty years.
5. **Compliance is easier.** FTC still applies, but not platform rules on top - which matters in banned categories.
6. **No spokesperson = sellable business.** An established outreach team is how Gym Launch was sellable.
7. **Hard to copy.** Many steps are invisible - how you scrape, how you personalize, what software distributes. Plus hiring, training and operating the team.

**Belief-breaking volume (p124):** to break past $1,000,000/month, one portfolio company automated scraping, crafting and sending. **One virtual assistant sends 2,000 emails per day** using multiple tools → **40 engaged leads/day** → 10% sold → **4 new customers per day.** Response rate dropped because personalization was stripped out; volume more than covered it. Business makes $20,000 per customer.

**Low-ticket warning (p120):** he couldn't make cold outreach profitable for a direct-to-consumer business - outreach teams are expensive and the ticket was too low. The fix: **use cold outreach to recruit affiliates instead of customers.** One affiliate sale can be worth thousands of customers. See `10-affiliates.md`.

| | |
|---|---|
| Who | Yourself |
| What | Hook + lead magnet / core offer |
| Where | Any private communication platform |
| To whom | List: scraped, bought, or software used |
| When | Every morning, 7 days a week |
| Why | Get leads to engage to sell stuff |
| How | Live calls, voicemail drops, email blasts, text blasts, direct messages, video messages, voice messages, direct mail, handwritten cards |
