# Affiliates

**The lead getter that made him the most money.** Two companies built on affiliates - ALAN and Prestige Labs - **more than $75,000,000 in revenue from over 5,000 affiliates.** (p225)

## What an affiliate is (p223, figure p223)
> "An affiliate is a lead-getter. They are an independent business that tells their audience to buy your stuff."

**Not a referral.** Two differences: they have their own business and do their own advertising, and they agree to offer your stuff to their engaged leads **in exchange for money, free stuff, or both.**

**The offer is different too.** You get affiliates by advertising and making them offers like customers - but affiliates want a unique offer: **"Instead of offering your product, you offer a fast, simple, and easy way to make commissions promoting it."** (p223)

## Why you want an army (p224, figure p224)
| | Scenario 1: you get customers | Scenario 2: you get affiliates |
|---|---|---|
| Same effort | sell 10 customers/month at $10,000 | sell 10 affiliates/month |
| Month 1 | $100,000 | $100,000 |
| Month 12 | **$1.2M total**, business plateaus | **$7.8M total**, and it grows every month after |
| | Low leverage | High leverage |

Each affiliate adds a **stream**, not a sale.

**ALAN, in the real world (p225).** Three levels of affiliates:
1. Agency super-affiliates who brought agency leads
2. Agencies who brought local business leads
3. Local businesses who brought end consumer leads

One super-affiliate added **10 agencies/month** → 10 agencies brought ~**50 local businesses/month** → those brought ~**2,500 leads/month**, worked at ~$5 a pop = **$12,500/month.** And it stacked: each super-affiliate brought $12,500 the first month, $25,000 the second, $37,500 the third. **With only a few agency super-affiliates they scaled to $1,700,000/month within six months of launching.**

**Prestige Labs, the launch (p222-223):** week four alone did **over $450,000** in sales. The trend held - $429k, $383k, $411k, $452k weeks. Over **300 orders per day across 400+ active affiliates.** And: *"I didn't advertise or sell any of the products at all. No paid ads. No sales team. Nothing. The affiliates did everything."*

## The six steps (p225)
1. Find your ideal affiliates
2. Make them an offer
3. Qualify them
4. Figure out what to pay them
5. Get them advertising
6. Keep them advertising

### Step 1: find them (p226)
> "The ideal affiliate has a business with a warm audience full of people like your customers."

If none come to mind, answer these about your **best** customers:
- What do they buy? → **Who provides that stuff?**
- Where do they go? → **What businesses are in those surrounding areas?**
- What do they like to do? → **Who provides those services?**
- Direct-to-consumer: what types of businesses do they work for? What jobs do they have? **The employers of your consumers make great affiliates.**

> "In a nutshell... Who's got my leads!?" (p226)

Starting ALAN he listed **200 products and services for agencies** and who delivered them, and they fell into categories: **softwares, products, equipment, services, groups they belong to, and events they attended.** He starts every affiliate hit list with those categories.

**A business in multiple categories has a high chance of having lots of good leads for you.** (p226)

**If you struggle: call your customers and ask them.** (p226)

### Step 2: make them an offer (p226-227)
Same structure as any ad - **call out, value elements, CTA.**

**Callouts for affiliates (p227):**
- The affiliate business owners themselves - *ATTENTION SPA OWNERS*
- The affiliate's customers - *Do you work with busy professionals who spend all day in meetings?*
- Results the affiliate promises - *To the heroes who heal the stress of others...*
- Products and services they deliver - *If you sell lotions or scented oils this is for you...*
- **Your own customers** - *Do you know anyone who owns a spa?*

**The value structure nearly all affiliate offers use (p227):**
> "Make more money from your current customers and get more leads than your current offer **(dream outcome)**... with a high chance of working since your customers already want the product **(perceived likelihood of achievement)**... without needing to build, deliver, or provide customer support for the product yourself **(effort and sacrifice)**... so you can start selling it tomorrow **(time delay)**."

### Step 3: qualify them - make them invest (p227-229, figures p228, p229)
> "nine times out of ten, if they pay, they'll pay attention." (p228)

Two ways, best used together:

**Way 1 - Make them a customer (p228).** Make them buy and preferably use the product to keep affiliate status. Lowest barrier that works. **"the more money an affiliate invests in your product, the more money they make."** And: *"If they don't believe in your stuff enough to buy it, they probably shouldn't sell it."*

> **Bulk purchases (p228).** Huge for Prestige Labs. "Once they bought a big package up front they started following through and winning." The phrasing: *"So you want anything extra or just go with the minimum order?"* By presenting a minimum, they'll at least buy that, and often more.

**Way 2 - Make them an expert (p229).** Charge for the onboarding and training that certifies them as a product expert. If they bought product to become an affiliate, that can be credit toward the certification.

**The pricing rule:** charge **10-20% of what the average active affiliate makes in their first twelve months.** Average affiliate makes $40,000/year → charge **$4,000-$8,000** to onboard and train them.
> "Too low, and you won't get them invested. Too high and you won't get enough affiliates. I found 10-20% maximizes the number of people who become active affiliates." (p229)

**If you're just starting:** use bulk purchasing for physical products, or use the warm-outreach ladder - **raise the minimum investment every 5 sign ups** until you hit the sweet spot.

**Tuning it (p229):** "If you don't get enough people to start, lower the commitment. If you don't get enough people to follow through, raise it."

### Step 4: what to pay them (p229-231, figure p229)
Two questions: **what they get paid for**, and **how much.**

**What for (p230):** ask "what exactly do I want the affiliate to do?" Then pay for that. He pays for **new customers and repeat customers.** With better tracking you can pay for earlier steps - lead magnets downloaded, appointments set - anything that reliably turns into sales.

**How much (p230):** base it on your **maximum allowable CAC.**
> $200 single-use product, $40 to fulfil = $160 left. Want 3:1 LTGP:CAC → three parts ($120) to the business, one part (**$40**) to the affiliate. That's the max you'll pay for an affiliate to get a new customer.

**Then split it into three tiers (p230-231):**
| Tier | Payout | Trigger |
|---|---|---|
| **Tier 1** | 25% of CAC = $10 | anyone who agrees to your initial terms (signs up, buys products or a certification) |
| **Tier 2** | 50% of CAC = $20 | **once they activate** - finish the certification, do a set number of posts and outreach, do a launch |
| **Tier 3** | 100% of CAC = $40 | **once they sustain performance** - e.g. maintain five customers/month on subscription |

**The hidden profit in tiering (p231):** the *average* payout comes in well below your maximum. If 20% of sales come from tier 1, 20% from tier 2 and 60% from tier 3, your **blended payout is $30, not $40** - which moves LTGP:CAC from **3:1 to 4:1.** "cutting marketing costs by 33% can translate into 10% to 20% more net profit at the end of the year." Use the leftover for contests, recruiting more affiliates, and incentivizing rising stars.

**Pay with product where possible (p231).** "They value it at retail, but it only costs you - your cost. A nice arbitrage of value." At low tiers you can compensate **exclusively** with free stuff. But as volume rises they'll want money - *cashing in 100 massages becomes unrealistic.*
> Prestige Labs: anyone who sold more than three packages a month got a free **$200 bundle** of their choosing, and became a sponsored athlete with free products for life as long as they kept three clients/month buying. **"Sell three to get it free."**

### Step 5: get them advertising - the launch (p232-234, figures p232, p233, p234)
> "good launches have the work done ahead of time. So do all the work for them. Then, they can plug and play." (p232)

**Whisper → Tease → Shout.** (This is how you launch *anything*, not just with affiliates - he put it here because he hasn't found a better way to activate them.)

**WHISPER - think "Call Outs."** Curiosity. Keep the product mysterious, hint at how big a deal it is, keep whispers short. Bonus points for behind-the-scenes of making it.
> "the longer something appears to take, the more an audience will value it... an audience will value a product that took ten years to make more than one that took ten days. So - show your work." (p233)

You can start **a few years out.**
*His book launch whisper phase:* posted content, reached out to friends, emailed the list, told potential affiliates about major updates. Showed which draft he was on, photos of printing drafts, the many versions of frameworks he drew, videos of editing early morning and late at night.

**Cadence: every 4-6 weeks until 60 days out, then every 2-3 weeks until 30 days out.** (p233)

**TEASE - think "Elements Of Value."** Start satisfying the curiosity. Reveal the product, make the launch date public, show the value elements using the **What-Who-When framework** (see `05-paid-ads.md`).
*His tease phase:* specific, hard information; advertised how the book satisfied the dream outcome of limitless leads, less work, done faster; showed dozens of examples of using it.

**Cadence: once per week until 14 days out, then twice per week until 3 days out.** (p234)

**SHOUT - think "Call to Action."** Specific actions to take at launch. Pound the audience with **bonuses, scarcity, urgency and guarantees around being "the first ones."**
*His shout phase:* short, sweet, clear reminders to register, plus reminders of the exclusive bonuses only for launch buyers.

**Cadence: at least twice a day starting 3 days out. On the day, every few hours until 2 hours out, then every 30 minutes until launch.** (p234)

**The best real-world example is movie releases (p234):** five-second trailers a year out, thirty seconds at ninety days, longer trailers as the date approaches. **Curiosity, then interest, then action.**

### Step 6: keep them advertising - integration (p235-238, figures p235, p236, p237)
> "In an ideal world, you sell an affiliate once and they send engaged leads for life. Integration gets us there." (p235)

Three ways, ordered easiest to hardest:

**1. Affiliates give your lead magnet away when somebody buys their stuff (p235-236, figure p235).** Your lead magnet makes *their* offer more valuable, so they can charge more and get more leads. Then you upsell your core offer.
> *Samples and trials:* you sell massages, recruit the personal training studio next door - everyone who buys training gets a free massage from you.
> *Reveal a problem:* a free or discounted posture assessment with every training package.
> *One step of a multi-step process:* you have a three-part treatment plan (massage, stretching, adjustments); the affiliate gives away step one free, you upsell the rest.
> *What he did:* gym affiliates gave a free nutrition consult to every new member; they upsold products at the consult.

> **White label lead magnets (p236).** "One of my favorite strategies is to let them use the lead magnets I've already made for my audience, for theirs." For gyms he made white-labelled (no logo) meal plans, grocery lists and food prep instructions. **"All they had to do was slap their logo on it."** Just make sure they agree with how you give value and understand your CTA.

**2. Affiliates sell your lead magnet (p237, figure p236).** They sell anything of yours that turns their customers into your customers - a book, an event, a service, software, a sample. **Giving them all the cash from selling a lead magnet you fulfil is all profit and no work for them** - an attractive proposition for any business. Your money comes from selling your main thing for more than the lead magnet cost to deliver. **And you don't split anything on your core offer.**
> *What he did:* gyms sold a nutrition consult with his team for $99-$199 and kept the money. *"If we were clever, we'd let them keep all the money. If we do, they'll send us even more leads."*

**3. Affiliates sell your core offer (p237-238, figure p237).** They sell it directly to their customers. For some affiliates this becomes their entire income. **They get a higher percentage of your LTGP, but you do nothing but deliver.**
> *What he did:* taught gyms to hold nutrition consultations with white-labelled products, then upsell supplements to their members, and split the money.

**Splitting terms (p237):** either split the upfront cash, all cash for a set period, or all cash forever. **"I prefer to pay forever so my affiliates stay motivated to keep my customers forever. And, I never cap payouts."**

**What he settled on (p238):** Strategy 1 twice a year as a big event, and Strategy 3 ongoing. Many portfolio businesses use Strategy 2.

## Three case studies (p238-240)
**1. National tax preparation services ($50M).** Prepares LLCs, bank accounts, articles of incorporation for first-time business owners. Doesn't compete with LegalZoom - **partners with people who train new entrepreneurs.** Offers every affiliate's customer a **free LLC setup** (a "high cost lead magnet").
- *Launch:* a big blast-off seminar to affiliates' audiences.
- *Integrate:* affiliates fold the free LLC into their core offer; his team then gets on the phone and sells what they need next - bookkeeping, tax prep.
- **Zero dollars on paid ads.** Costs are the lead magnet delivery and a percentage of every first sale.

**2. Prestige Labs (his supplement company).** Gym Launch sells and trains gym owners; those gyms have active-adult customers. So **Gym Launch is a perfect affiliate for Prestige Labs.**
- *Launch:* give gym owners materials to re-engage current and former customers - warm outreach and free content for a **free 28-day challenge**; gym owners sell supplements to use with the program.
- *Integrate:* supplements sold to every new gym member. New member buys a package → nutrition orientation → **$50-$1000 of supplements.** Gym signs 20 clients/month with 70% buying = **14 new customers per month per gym.** Then: **4,000 gyms × 14 sales/month × $200 average order.**

**3. Chiropractors (local business).** Go to high-volume businesses full of people needing adjustments - a gym fits.
- *Launch:* gym owner promotes a three-hour workshop on correct exercises and posture, free or $29-$99 per person, and **the chiropractor splits the money - hint, give the affiliate 100% and they'll want to do it more.** 30 people at $99 = **$2,970 profit for the gym for a few emails and posts.** The chiropractor soft-pitches at the workshop.
- *Integrate:* the gym includes one to two adjustments with every new membership. Raises the gym's value versus the guy down the street, signals they prioritize member safety. **Every new gym member becomes a chiropractic lead.** Repeat across thirty gyms.

**Employees are leads too (p240).** Companies that hire a lot of people make great affiliates - **HUGE for direct to consumer and wildly underused.** Every new hire gets a free massage in their onboarding packet; free massages to employees at lunch. They get free value, you get free leads, and since you're not in the same business there's no competition risk.

## The returns math (p240-241)
**Affiliates work differently.** You don't make money back from the affiliate - you make it back from **the customers they bring.** So compare the cost to get an affiliate against the gross profit of all the customers they send.

Worked (p241):
- CAC to get an affiliate = **$4,000**
- Average affiliate sells $10,000/month and stays 12 months = **$120,000 total sales**
- Widgets have 75% gross margins → $30,000 cost of goods → **$90,000 gross profit**
- Pay affiliates **40% of gross profit** = $36,000
- Leftover: $120,000 − $30,000 − $36,000 = **$54,000**
- $54,000 ÷ $4,000 = **12.5:1**

**If your actual affiliate LTGP:CAC is under 3, three fixes (p241):**
1. **Lower CAC** - get affiliates for less (better ads, offer, sales process)
2. **Increase LTGP and decrease CAC** - get more to *activate* (better launch process)
3. **Increase LTGP** - make them worth more (better integration process)

**Complexity warning (p242):** with affiliates you have at least two layers of customers - your customers and the people who get you customers. Super-affiliates add a third.

## The scaling sequence (p243)
1. Advertise your affiliate offer until you get **ten to twenty affiliates**
2. Get results with them and use their feedback to work the kinks out of the offer, terms, launches and integration strategy
3. **Turn their results into your first batch of affiliate lead magnets**, then scale

## The closing frame (p242)
> "there are two ways to create a compounding business. You can find more people that never stop buying your stuff or you can find more people who never stop selling it for you. Referrals are the former. Affiliates are the latter."

> "You are only as good as the goodwill you have with your affiliate partners."
