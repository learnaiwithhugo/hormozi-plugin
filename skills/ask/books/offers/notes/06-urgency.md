# Urgency

**Scarcity is a function of quantity. Urgency is a function of time.** (p111, highlighted)
Urgency limits *when* people can sign up, not how many. (p111, highlighted: "where you only limit when people can sign up, rather than how many")

**"Deadlines. Drive. Decisions."** (p111, and p113 highlighted: "Deadlines drive decisions")

## The four ethical ways

### 1. Cohort-based rolling urgency (p111-112)
Start clients on a regular cadence, even in unlimited numbers.
> **"If you sign up today, I can get you in with our next group that kicks off on Monday, otherwise you'll have to wait until our next kickoff date."** (p111, highlighted)

Juiced version: *"I actually had a client who signed up a few weeks ago drop out, so I have an opening for our next cohort that kicks off Monday. If you're pretty sure you're gonna do this sooner or later, might as well get in now so you can start reaping the rewards sooner rather than paying the same and waiting."*

The less frequently you kick off, the more powerful it is. It also gives you a choreographed onboarding, which matters more as you scale.

### 2. Rolling seasonal urgency (p113)
Real countdowns and real dates. **If they aren't real you lose credibility and look like every other wannabe marketer.**

Same promotion, new wrapper each month:
- New Year Promotion ends Jan 30
- Valentines Lovers Promo ends Feb 28
- Sexy By Spring Special ends March 31
- Fools in Love April Promo ends April 30

**Pro tip, local businesses (p113):** this is the number one strategy for local. They must vary marketing more often than national advertisers. A new wrapper with a date on the same core service beats the "same old" campaign consistently.

### 3. Pricing or bonus-based urgency (p113-114)
Attach the urgency to the **promotion, discount or bonus** rather than the service itself. A roofer will obviously still roof your house next month, so it would be a lie to say otherwise. But the promotion genuinely ends.
> *"Yes, let's get you started today so you can take advantage of the discount you came in for. I'm not sure how long we'll be running it as we change them every 4 weeks or so, and this is one of the better ones we've run in a while."*

**Pro tip, clean your pipeline with every price change (p114, highlighted):** *"The price is going up! So get in now!"* **Never raise your prices without letting people know.** It shows strength and pulls in cash from fence-sitters.

### 4. Exploding opportunity (p114)
Some opportunities genuinely decay: an arbitrage window, a platform before competitors arrive, an exploding job offer. Every second of delay costs them disproportionately. Emphasise it when it's real.

## The objection: "won't I lose sales by turning business away?" (p112)
It's a fear and it's unfounded. **The biggest sales in a week-long campaign happen in the last 4 hours of the last day, up to 50-60% of total.** The last 3% of the time produces 50-60% of the sales. Completely illogical, unmistakably human.

You make more from the many who act than you lose from the few who "miss out," because those people were never going to buy anyway. They didn't buy with their feet to the fire, so why would they without?

## If someone wants in right after a cohort starts (p112)
1. Offer a fast personalised onboarding as a "bonus" for signing up today, and get them in, or
2. (his preference) Explain the next group starts soon, so they get more time to review materials and talk to their team or family, plus a longer payment plan you can only offer because the start date is far out. An advantage most clients don't get.

**"You always have the advantage because you are the one who calls the shots."**
