# Diagnosis: what's actually broken

Alex diagnoses before he prescribes. Work down this list in order. **The order matters** - fixing something low on the list while something high is broken is wasted effort.

## 1. Is the market wrong?
`Starving Crowd > Offer Strength > Persuasion Skills` (p36)

Check the four indicators (see 01-market-selection):
- Massive pain?
- Purchasing power?
- Easy to target?
- Growing?

**If the market is shrinking, nothing else in this book works.** That was Lloyd: great product, zero-risk offer, natural salesman, dying market. Same man in a growing market did millions a month within five months.

Symptom: everything looks right and nothing sells.

## 2. Is the offer a commodity?
Test: would a prospect look at yours and a competitor's and think *"these are pretty much the same, I'll take the cheaper one"*? If yes, you've been commoditized. (p25)

Symptom: constant price objections, being asked to match a competitor, thin margins.

Fix: rebuild it so it cannot be compared. Not "better." **Incomparable.** (see 04-offer-construction)

## 3. Which value-equation variable is weakest?
Score the offer 0-1 on each (see 03-value-equation):
- Dream Outcome
- Perceived Likelihood of Achievement
- Time Delay
- Effort & Sacrifice

Whichever is lowest is where the work is. **If the weak one is on the bottom half (time or effort), that's the highest-leverage and most defensible fix**, because anyone can make a bigger promise but almost nobody removes the work.

Symptom by variable:
- Weak dream outcome → "I don't really need this"
- Weak likelihood → "will this actually work for me?"
- Weak time → "I'll get to it later"
- Weak effort → "that sounds like a lot"

## 4. Is the price too low?
**99% of businesses need to raise their prices to grow, not lower them.** (p52)

Symptoms of underpricing: bad clients who are never satisfied, no margin to deliver properly, poor client results, your own sales conviction dropping.

Rule: raise value first, then price. Never discount the core offer; add bonuses instead. (see 02-pricing, 07-bonuses)

## 5. Is a single unsolved problem killing the sale?
**"One single item becomes the reason someone doesn't buy."** (p87, highlighted)

Ask what the recurring objection is, then build the solution for it once and never lose that objection again. That's the eating-out guide story.

## 6. Is risk unreversed?
Changing nothing but the guarantee can 2-4x conversion. (p124) If there is no guarantee, or a vanilla one, that is usually the cheapest available win. (see 08-guarantees)

## 7. Is demand being satisfied too fast?
If sales fall off after each promotion, you're likely selling the whole pyramid at the low price and leaving zero pent-up demand. (p100-101, see 05-scarcity)

**Hormozi Law: the longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make.**

## 8. Is the offer just fatigued?
Only after everything above. Change in order: creative → body copy → headline/wrapper → duration → enhancer → monetization. (see 09-naming)

## Questions Alex asks before he answers anything
1. What are you selling, and who to?
2. What do you charge right now, and how did you arrive at that number?
3. What's the dream outcome for the buyer, in their words?
4. What's the number one reason people say no?
5. How many people say yes out of how many conversations?
6. What does it cost you to deliver one more customer?
7. What guarantee do you offer, if any?

He does not need all seven to give a useful answer. If the person hasn't given context, he gives the framework straight, then names the one or two things he'd need to make it specific.
