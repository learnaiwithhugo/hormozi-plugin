# Building the offer

**Figure p74 draws the whole process. Send it when walking someone through this.**

## The five steps
1. **Identify the dream outcome**
2. **List every problem** standing in the way
3. **Turn every problem into a solution**
4. **Create the delivery vehicles** (the "how")
5. **Trim and stack** into the final high-value deliverable

## Before you start: convergent vs divergent (p70-73)
Convergent thinking: many variables, one right answer (maths). Divergent: many variables, many right answers. **Offer creation is divergent.** The brick exercise (120 seconds, list every use of a brick) exists to force this. Most people list 5-8. The point is to keep going past where it feels finished.

## Step 1: Dream outcome (p75, highlighted)
Hormozi's gym: everyone sold $99/mo bootcamps. He sold **"Lose 20lbs in 6 weeks."**
- Big dream outcome: lose 20lbs
- Decreased time delay: 6 weeks

**"I wasn't selling my membership anymore. I wasn't selling the plane flight. I was selling the vacation."** (p75)

## Step 2: List problems (p75-77, figure p80)
Walk the sequence the customer actually experiences. For each core thing they must do, list every reason they wouldn't be able to do it or keep doing it.

Use the four value drivers as the prompt for each problem (p77):
1. Dream Outcome: "this will not be financially worth it"
2. Likelihood: "it won't work for me specifically, external factors will get in my way" (the most service-specific bucket)
3. Effort & Sacrifice: "this will be too hard, confusing, I'll suck at it"
4. Time: "this takes too long, I'm too busy, it won't be convenient"

His weight-loss example produced **16 core problems with 2-4 sub-problems each, so 32 to 64 problems total.** That is the right density. **Highlighted (p77):** "go ahead and list out all the problems your prospect has."

**The more problems you find, the more you get to solve.** (p77, highlighted)

## Step 3: Solutions list (p77-79, figure p81)
Mechanically reverse each problem into solution language. Add "How to" and flip it.

| Problem | Solution |
|---|---|
| Buying healthy food is hard, confusing, I'll suck at it | How to make buying healthy food easy and enjoyable, so anyone can do it (especially busy moms) |
| ...takes too much time | How to buy healthy food quickly |
| ...is expensive | How to buy healthy food for less than your current grocery bill |
| ...is undoable if I travel | How to get healthy food when travelling |

**Highlighted (p79):** "if only one of these needs is missing in a solution, it can cause someone not to buy."
**Highlighted (p79):** "Just get all the problems down then turn them into solutions."

## Step 4: Delivery vehicles - the how (p84-87)
For each solution, list **every** way you could deliver it. Push past what you're willing to do.

**The question to ask (p84, highlighted):** *"What could you do that someone would immediately say, 'All that? Seriously? Yes, I'm in.'"*

Group by how many people you serve at once: 1-on-1, small group, one-to-many.

### The Delivery Cube (p86-87, figure p86) - the cheat codes
- **Personal attention:** one-on-one / small group / one to many
- **Effort level:** DIY (they figure it out) / DWY (you teach them) / DFY (you do it)
- **If live, what medium:** in person, phone, email, text, Zoom, chat
- **If recorded, what format:** audio, video, written
- **Speed and convenience:** 24/7, 9-5, reply within 5 min, within an hour, within 24 hrs
- **The 10x / 1/10th test:** If they paid 10x (or $100,000), what would I provide? If they paid 1/10th and I had to make it MORE valuable, how? Stretching in both directions produces wildly different answers.

**Reminder (p84):** you only do this once, for a product that may last years. It is the highest-leverage work there is.

## Step 5a: Trim (p88-89)
You will have a gigantic list. Now cut by **cost to you vs value to them**:
1. Remove **high cost, low value** first
2. Then remove **low cost, low value**
3. What remains: **low cost / high value** and **high cost / high value**

To judge "high value," run it back through the value equation (p89): will this person 1) financially value it, 2) believe they're more likely to succeed, 3) feel it takes much less effort and sacrifice, 4) get there with far less time investment?

**Focus on high-value one-to-many solutions.** They have the biggest gap between cost and value. His meal-plan spreadsheet took ~100 hours once, then produced personalised plans in 15 minutes each, forever. High value, low cost. (This is why software is valuable.)

## The Sales to Fulfillment Continuum (p82-83, figure p82)
Do less and it gets harder to sell but easier to fulfil. Do more and it gets easy to sell but hard to fulfil. **Find the sweet spot: sells very well AND is easy to fulfil** (p83, highlighted).

**The mantra (p83, highlighted):** *"Create flow. Monetize flow. Then add friction."* Generate demand first. Get them saying yes. **Only then** add friction or offer less for the same price.

Practical order: over-deliver like crazy at first to create cash flow, then use the cash flow to fix operations. You may not change what you offer at all, just build systems that deliver the same value for less cost.

## Step 5b: Stack (p90-93)
Put the surviving pieces together, and present each as:
`Problem → Solution wording → Sexier name for the bundle → the actual delivery vehicles underneath → a dollar value`

Real example (p91):
> **Buying food → How anyone can buy food fast, easy, cheaply → "Foolproof Bargain Grocery System"** ... that'll save hundreds of dollars per month and take less time than your current shopping routine ($1,000 value)
> a) 1-on-1 Nutrition Orientation b) Recorded grocery tour c) DIY Grocery Calculator d) Weekly lists e) Bargain shopping training f) Grocery Buddy System g) Pre-made insta-cart carts h) Weekly text check-in

His final stack: **total value $4,351, all for $599.**

## Solve every problem (p87-88)
**Highlighted (p87):** "one single item becomes the reason someone doesn't buy."
The eating-out story: he refused to solve "but I eat out for lunch every day" and lost sales for months. He caved once out of rent desperation, made an eating-out guide, closed the sale, and never lost that objection again. **Don't get romantic about how you want to solve the problem. Find a way to solve every problem a prospect presents.**

## The recap to quote (p90, all highlighted)
1. Figured out the prospect's dream outcome
2. Listed all the obstacles they're likely to encounter (our opportunities for value)
3. Listed those obstacles as solutions
4. Figured out all the different ways we could deliver those solutions
5a. Trimmed to only highest value, lowest cost to us
5b. Put all the bundles together into the ultimate high value deliverable
