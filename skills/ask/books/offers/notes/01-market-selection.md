# Market selection

**This outranks the offer. The offer outranks persuasion.** (p36)
`Starving Crowd (market) > Offer Strength > Persuasion Skills`

A "great" rating in a higher-priority slot beats anything lower on the list. A "bad" market stops everything: nothing else matters if the market is dying.

## The hot dog stand (p31, highlighted)
> "If you were going to open a hotdog stand, and you could only have one advantage over your competitors, which would it be?"
> ... "A starving crowd."

You could have the worst hot dogs, terrible prices and a terrible location, but if you're the only stand in town when the football game breaks out, you sell out. (p31, all highlighted)

Covid toilet paper: no offer, atrocious pricing, no sales pitch, $100 a roll. That is the value of a starving crowd.

## The Lloyd story - the market kills you quietly (p32)
Great product, zero-risk revshare offer, natural salesman. Business declined anyway. His market (newspapers) was shrinking 25% a year. Same man pivoted to automated mask manufacturing during Covid and did **millions per month within five months**. Same skill set, zero experience in the new market. (p32, heavily highlighted)

**Diagnosis rule:** when someone's business is failing and their product, offer and sales are all good, look at the market before anything else.

## The four indicators (p33-35)
1. **Massive pain** - they must not want but desperately *need* it. Degree of pain sets the price you can charge. **"The pain is the pitch."** (p34, highlighted)
2. **Purchasing power** - they must be able to afford it. (The resume guy whose market was all unemployed.) **Highlighted (p34):** "your audience needs to be able to afford the service you're charging them for."
3. **Easy to target** - associations, mailing lists, groups, channels. If finding them is needles in a haystack, the offer never gets seen.
4. **Growing** - a growing market is a tailwind, a declining one is a headwind.

## The three markets that always exist (p35)
**Health, Wealth, Relationships.** Find a smaller subgroup inside one of those that is growing and easy to target.

Definition of a "normal market": growing at the same rate as the marketplace, with unmet needs falling into health, wealth or relationships. A normal market is enough. You do not need a great one.

## Commit to the niche (p37)
- "Don't make me niche slap you."
- **"The grass is never greener once you get to the other side."** (p37, highlighted)
- Switching markets restarts the clock. You will fail far longer by changing who you serve than by sticking.
- "Riches are in the niches" - because niching multiplies what you can charge.

100x the price for near-identical content. **"You can literally charge 100x more for the exact same product."** (p38, highlighted, credited to Dan Kennedy)

## When to broaden (p38)
Under $10M/yr: almost always stay narrow. Broadening is a problem for later, and even then you go up-market, down-market or adjacent rather than wide.

## Checklist before building anything
- [ ] Is the pain acute, not a nice-to-have?
- [ ] Can they pay the price I need?
- [ ] Can I reliably put a message in front of them?
- [ ] Is the market flat or growing?
- [ ] Have I picked ONE and committed?
