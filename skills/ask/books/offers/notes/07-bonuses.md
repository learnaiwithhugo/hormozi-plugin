# Bonuses

## The core principle
**A single offer is less valuable than the same offer broken into its component parts and stacked as bonuses.** (p116)

You already built the pieces in trim and stack. Bonuses are about **what order you reveal them in**. Anchor the price to the core offer, then keep expanding value until the price-to-value discrepancy is too big to bear.

The infomercial logic: sell one knife for $38.95, then include 37 other knives, sharpeners, pans and guarantees. They establish the price, then expand on it until passing feels stupid. Every second of air time costs money, so they wouldn't do it if it didn't work.

## Never discount, always add (p117, Pro Tip)
**Whenever trying to close a deal, never discount the main offer.** It teaches customers your prices are negotiable. Adding bonuses to increase value is far superior to cutting price. It puts you in a position of strength and goodwill rather than weakness.

## When to present them, 1-on-1 (p117, highlighted)
- **Ask for the sale first, before offering bonuses.** If they say yes, then *after* they've signed up, tell them about the extra bonuses. Creates a wow experience and reinforces the decision.
- **If they don't buy after the first ask,** present a bonus that matches their specific perceived obstacle, then ask again. Agree with them, add the bonus, ask "fair enough?" People have a hard time rejecting reciprocity.
- Each deliverable from trim and stack is now **"weaponized and presented at the perfect time"** (p117, highlighted).

## The 12 bonus rules (p118-119)
1. Always offer them
2. Give a special name with a benefit in the title
3. Tell them: how it relates to their issue; what it is; how you discovered it or what you had to do to create it; how it will specifically make their life faster, easier, less effort (the value equation again)
4. Provide proof it's valuable (a stat, a past client, personal experience)
5. Paint a vivid mental image of life having already used it
6. Always ascribe a price tag and justify it
7. **Tools and checklists beat additional trainings** - lower effort and time for them, so higher value
8. Each should kill a specific concern or obstacle in their mind
9. Or solve the **next** problem before they even hit it
10. **The value of the bonuses should eclipse the value of the core offer.** Subconsciously it says the core offer must be worth even more
11. Add scarcity or urgency to the bonuses themselves:
    - *Scarcity:* "only people who sign up get Bonus 1, 2, 3, never for sale anywhere else" / "I have 3 tickets left to my $5,000 virtual event, buy this and get one"
    - *Urgency:* "if you buy today I'll add XYZ that normally costs $1,000, free, because I reward action takers"
12. **A guarantee itself can be a bonus:** "I want to remove any fear about deciding today, so if you move forward today I'll also give you a 30-day money back guarantee, which I don't normally offer"

## What should be a bonus vs part of the core offer (p123)
**Wow factor.** Something you wouldn't want someone to miss. When you're providing a lot of stuff, valuable nuggets get lost in the mix. Pull out the most distinct ones that could almost stand alone, especially things short in length but high in value. A checklist or infographic condenses a lot into a small space; nobody pays a lot for a "product launch map," but as a bonus it's perceived as very valuable.

## Advanced: other people's products and services (p119-121)
Get adjacent (non-competing) businesses to give you their products or services as bonuses, in exchange for exposure to your customers. Free marketing for them, free high-value bonuses for you.

Pain clinic example: free massages, two free chiropractic adjustments ($100), food company discounts ($50), braces and orthotics discounts ($150), a free PT session and pool month ($100), pharmacy discounts ($100/mo). On a $400 offer, **the bonuses alone are worth more than the price.**

Then go further: negotiate a **group discount AND a commission to yourself**. Chiro pays $100 per referral, orthotics $100, health club $50, pharmacy $100. Now the $400 offer can generate an extra $350 in pure profit. You're negotiating with the purchasing power of all your customers at once.

## The summary actions (p122)
1. Create checklists, tools, swipe files, scripts and templates: things that take lots of time to create once but are easy to use, and can be given away endlessly
2. Record every workshop, webinar, event and interview and bank them as bonuses to deploy against a perceived obstacle
3. Proactively negotiate group discounts and referral commissions with adjacent businesses that solve the next need your customer will have

**Author note (p122):** the longer you're in business, the bigger the vault of bonus assets. Keep them in your back pocket to sprinkle in and close a deal. Information products fit perfectly: high perceived value, low cost, zero operational effort.
