# Scarcity

**Scarcity is a function of quantity. Urgency is a function of time.** (p111, highlighted)

## Why it works
**Highlighted (p106):** "fear of loss is stronger than desire for gain."
**Highlighted (p99):** "Desire comes from not getting what you want." And: "we only want things we do not have. As soon as we have them, our desire for them disappears."

George at the Arnold fundraiser (p97, highlighted): **"When demand increases, cut supply."** They cut ticket numbers and raised price from $15,000 to $25,000, and raised an extra $1M before the event even started. The night raised **$5.4M from 100 people ($54,000 a head)**.

**Highlighted (p97):** "People want what they can't have. People want what other people want. People want things only a select few have access to."

## The supply/demand mechanics (p99-101, figures p98-p101)
All marketing exists to move the supply and demand curve. Increase demand to sell more units; decrease supply to sell them for more. The perfect profit combination is **lots of demand and very little supply, or perceived supply**.

Demand is **fractal, roughly 80/20**: about one fifth of prospects will pay five times the price.

Worked example (p100-101):
- Scenario one: sell 10 units at $500 = $5,000. Everyone who wants it gets it. **Zero pent-up demand.** Next promotion sells fewer.
- Scenario two: sell 2 units at $5,000 = $10,000. Eight people still want it. **Pent-up demand.** Next time you open three spots at the same price and sell them all.

**Hormozi Law (p101, highlighted):** *"The longer you delay the ask, the bigger the ask you can make. The longer the runway, the bigger the plane that can take off."*

**Highlighted (p102):** "if you satisfy all the demand, you will kill your golden goose, and not know where your next meal will come from."

## Why experts charge egregious rates (p103-105)
Implied demand. **Highlighted (p103):** "People assume that there is a lot of demand for their time, and therefore not a big supply of it. As a result, it must be expensive."

Rarity compounds with value. Many people can answer "how do I make $10,000/mo." Almost nobody can answer "how do I add $5M in profit without adding product lines." (p104)

**Highlighted (p105):** *"The person who needs the exchange less always has the upper hand."*

## Three types of scarcity (p106, highlighted)
1. Limited supply of seats/slots, in general or over X period
2. Limited supply of bonuses
3. Never available again

## For physical products (p106)
Limited releases on flavours, colours, designs, sizes. **You should always sell out** (p106, highlighted). Better to sell out consistently than over-order and kill the scarcity. Roughly once a month is the sweet spot.

**And announce that you sold out.** That is half of what makes it work: it is social proof, and the choice being taken away increases desire.

Chanel sends only 1-2 of each piece to each store, so every store differs and every item is the last or second-to-last in stock.

## For services - three caps (p107, all highlighted)
1. **Total business cap** - "Only accepting X clients." *"My agency only will service twenty-five customers total. Period."* Creates a waiting list; the moment a door opens, price resistance disappears. Periodically increase capacity 10-20%, then cap again.
2. **Growth rate cap** - "Only accepting X clients per week." *"We only accept 5 new clients per week and we already have the first 3 spots taken. I have 6 more calls this week."*
3. **Cohort cap** - "Only accepting X clients per class or cohort." *"We take on 100 clients 4 times a year. We open the doors then close them."*

## Honest scarcity, the most ethical version (p108-109)
You genuinely couldn't handle 1,000 clients tomorrow. So **define the number you're actually willing to take in a period and advertise that** (p108, highlighted). Telling people you're three-quarters to capacity this week, or 81% to capacity overall, moves them. It carries implied social proof. **Only you get to decide where "full" is.**

## Pro tips
- **Higher-ticket upsells (p108, highlighted):** scarcity tactics work especially well on workshops, trainings, events, seminars, consulting. Always have fewer spots than you think you can sell, so next time everyone remembers you sold out fast. This compounds.
- **Extreme scarcity (p109, highlighted):** *"If you don't hate money, sell a very limited supply of 1-on-1 access."* Create a very exclusive service level based on access to you, cap it tiny, price it very high. Pick the medium you don't hate (he hates email, doesn't mind Zoom).
- **Once you're out, you can never come back (p110):** cap the level and say leavers can't return. Works best with small groups; loses teeth as groups get big.
- Scarcity works on **free** things too: a lead magnet capped at 20 downloads a week becomes desirable rather than a "neat free download," and gets consumed when it's finally obtained (p108).
