# Guarantees

## Why it matters more than almost anything else
**The single greatest objection for any product or service is risk.** Risk that it doesn't do what it's supposed to do for them.

**Highlighted (p124):** "reversing risk is an immediate way to make any offer more attractive."

Changing nothing about what you sell, only the quality of the guarantee, can **2-4x the conversion of an offer.** (p124)

**"You must always hit your guarantee hard, even if you don't have one. Say it boldly and give the reason why."** (p124)

## The shape of a real guarantee (p125, figure p125)
> **"If you don't achieve X in Y time, we will Z."**

What gives it teeth is the **"or what"**. Without deciding what you'll do if they don't get the result, it sounds weak and diluted.

- Bad: "We will get you 20 clients guaranteed."
- Better: "You will get 20 clients in your first 30 days, or we give you your money back plus your advertising dollars spent with us."

## The refund maths - do this before you get emotional (p124-125)
100 sales, 5 refunds (5%) = **95 net sales**
Guarantee: 130 sales, 13 refunds (10%) = **117 net sales**
117 / 95 = **1.23x, a 23% increase**, and it all falls to the bottom line.

For a guarantee not to be worth it, the increase in sales would have to be **100% offset** by refunds: a 5-point rise in sales needing a 5-point rise in refunds, which usually means a doubling of the refund rate. Unlikely. **The stronger the guarantee, the higher the net increase in purchases, even as refunds rise.**

## The warning (p125, highlighted)
**"People who buy because of guarantees can become very shitty customers."** Someone who only buys because of a guarantee may not put in the work. The fix: **tie your guarantee to the things they need to do to be successful** (p125, highlighted). That helps everyone.

**Pro tip, high-cost services (p125):** if fulfilment costs you a lot, use a conditional or anti-guarantee. Otherwise you eat the refund AND the cost of fulfilling.

## The four types (p126)
1. **Unconditional** - strongest. **"Basically a trial where they pay first then see if they like it"** (p126, highlighted). Gets a lot more people to buy; you will get some refunds.
2. **Conditional** - terms and conditions attached. Where you get creative. Aim for **"better than money back."** If you know the key actions that drive success, make those the conditions. In a perfect world 100% of customers qualify but none want it, because they got the result.
3. **Anti-guarantee** - **"all sales are final"** (p126, highlighted). You must own it with a creative reason why, usually showing real exposure or vulnerability on your part.
4. **Implied** - performance-based (p127, highlighted). Revshare, profit-share, triggers, ratchets, bonuses. If I don't perform, I don't get paid.

## Stacking guarantees (p127)
- Unconditional 30-day no-questions-asked, PLUS a conditional triple-your-money-back at 90 days.
- Or two conditional guarantees on sequential outcomes: "$10,000 by 60 days, $30,000 by 90 days, as long as you do 1, 2 and 3." This future-paces them into an outcome they now believe is far more likely.

## Name it something cool (p128, highlighted Pro Tip)
- Generic (bad): "30 Day Money Back Satisfaction Guarantee"
- Creative imagery (good): "In 30 days, if you wouldn't jump into shark infested waters to get our product back, we will return every dollar you paid."
- Creative imagery (great): the **"Club a Baby Seal Guarantee"** - after 30 days of using our services, if you wouldn't club a baby seal to stay on as a customer, you don't pay a penny.

## The menu of guarantees (p127-135)

**Unconditional**
- *No questions asked refund:* full refund, 50% refund, refund of ad spend and ancillary costs, you pay for a competitor's program instead, or their money plus an extra $1,000. Simple, and risky.
- *Satisfaction-based refund* (p129, highlighted): if at any time they're not satisfied with the **level of service**, they can request a refund. His actual weight-loss guarantee. Two people took it out of 4,000 sales in three and a half years. Pairs with the best-case/worst-case close: *"Best case you get the body of your dreams. Worst case you tell me I suck, I write you a check, and you get six weeks of free training. Both are risk free. The only thing guaranteed not to help you is walking out of here today."*

**Conditional**
- *Outsized refund* (p130, highlighted): **double or triple their money back**, or a no-strings payment far above what they paid. For high margins, with a consumption condition. The affiliate marketer who offered to buy your ecommerce store for $25,000 claimed **$3M in extra sales on a $2,997 course, and only paid out 10 of them: $250,000 of refunds generating $2.75M**.
- *Service guarantee* (p131, highlighted): **you keep working for them free of charge until X is achieved.** His personal favourite. Guarantees the outcome, removes time from the equation, and you never risk the money. Make it conditional on key actions: attending calls, showing up, reporting data. **He has yet to have a single client actually take someone up on it.**
- *Modified service guarantee* (p132, highlighted): another defined period of service free of charge, generally at least twice the original duration. Easier starting point than the open-ended version.
- *Credit-based* (p132, highlighted): money back as credit toward any service you offer. Best on upsells.
- *Personal service* (p132, highlighted): **you work with them one-on-one, free, until they hit X.** One of the strongest in existence. Definitely add conditions.
- *Hotel + airfare perks* (p133): reimburse the product plus hotel and airfare. Great for in-person events.
- *Wage-payment* (p133): pay their hourly rate if they don't find the session valuable.
- *Release of service* (p133): let them out of the contract free of charge. Free to offer if you don't enforce contracts anyway.
- *Delayed second payment* (p133): you don't bill again until they get their first outcome. Focuses your team on activating the client.
- *First outcome* (p134): you keep paying their ancillary costs (ad spend, hotel) until they hit the first outcome.

**Anti-guarantee**
- *All sales are final* (p134): for something that once seen cannot be unseen. A line of code, a proprietary process, your actual funnels and metrics. Lean into it: *"We're going to be exposing the inner workings of our business, as a result, all sales are final."* Also works on high-ticket, high-customisation work: *"If you're the type of customer who needs a guarantee before taking a jump, then you are not the type of person we want to work with."*

**Implied / performance** (p135)
- Performance: only pay per sale, per show, per lb lost
- Revshare: 10% of top line, 20% profit share, 25% of revenue growth from baseline
- Profit-share, ratchets (10% over X, 20% over Y), bonuses and triggers
- **Highlighted (p135):** "if you don't make money, you don't have to pay me."
- His view: this is possibly **THE most desirable setup**. It makes you accountable to results and weeds out low performers. Drawbacks are tracking and collection. Can be paired with a minimum: "the greater of $1,000 or 10% of revenue generated."

## Building your own (p136, highlighted)
**"Identify a client's biggest fears, pain, and perceived obstacles. What do they not want to have happen if they pay you? What are they most afraid of?"** Reverse their fears into a guarantee. Include the time, emotion and outside costs. The more specific and creative, the better.

## The limit
**Guarantees are enhancers. They can enhance the attraction of any offer, but they cannot make a business.** If a guarantee covers up a poor sales team or a poor product, it backfires into refunds.

**His advice (p136):** start with service-based guarantees or performance partnerships. That makes all sales final (no refund fear), and commits you to your customers' results. From there either keep it and scale, or move up to less restrictive guarantees to increase volume.
