# Pricing

## The rule
**Charge as high a price as you can say out loud without cracking a smile.** (Dan Kennedy, p42)

**Highlighted (p54):** "the goal should be to charge as much money for your products or services as humanly possible."
**Highlighted (p54):** "only a select few can charge these rates and get people to say yes."

## Price to value discrepancy (p45, figure p45)
People buy to get a deal. They buy when **what they get (value) is worth more than what they give (price)** (p45, highlighted). The moment value dips below price, they stop buying. That crossing point is the **point of cancellation**.

Two ways to widen the gap:
1. Lower the price - the wrong move almost always. You can only go down to $0; you can go infinitely up.
2. **Raise the value, then raise the price.** They still get a steal: think buying $100,000 of value for $10,000.

**Highlighted (p45):** "don't compete on price."
**Highlighted (p45, Dan Kennedy):** "There is no strategic benefit to being the second cheapest in the marketplace, but there is for being the most expensive."

## How most people price, and why it's broken (p46)
1. Look at the marketplace
2. See what everyone else offers
3. Take the average
4. Go slightly below to stay "competitive"
5. Provide what competitors offer plus "a little more"
6. Land on "more for less"

**The competitors they are copying are dead broke.** Pricing at market means pricing for market efficiency, where everyone ends up making just enough to keep going.

## The Virtuous Cycle of Price (p48, figure p48)
**When you DECREASE price you:** (p48, highlighted)
- Decrease clients' emotional investment (it didn't cost them much)
- Decrease clients' perceived value (it can't be good if it's cheap)
- Decrease clients' results (they don't value it, so they aren't invested)
- Attract the worst clients, **"who are never satisfied until your service is free"** (p49, highlighted)
- Destroy the margin you need to actually deliver an exceptional experience

**When you RAISE price you:** (p49)
- Increase emotional investment
- Increase perceived value
- Increase results
- Attract the best clients, who are easiest to satisfy and cost less to fulfil
- Multiply margin, which funds systems, people and scale

## Higher price literally means higher value (p49-50)
Blind wine tasting: same wine poured three times at three prices. Tasters rated them in price order. **Highlighted (p50):** "raising your prices can directly enhance the value you provide." And: **"People want to buy expensive things. They just need a reason."**

The target isn't slightly above market. It's high enough that the prospect thinks *"this is so much more expensive, there must be something entirely different going on here."* That is how you create a category of one and make monopoly profits.

## The moral argument
If a customer must do something to get the result, they have to be invested. Price it so it **stings a little**. Those who pay the most pay the most attention. Better adherence, better results, so you genuinely deliver more value than competitors.

## The cost-to-charge gap (p54, highlighted)
"You need to have a big discrepancy between what something costs you and what you charge for it. It is the only way to be unreasonably successful."
Think up to 100x cost, not 2-3x. And **"if you provide enough value it should still always be a steal for the prospect"** (p54, highlighted).

## The conviction problem
**Highlighted (p51):** "This was possible because my conviction was stronger than their skepticism."
To charge big-ticket you must **outwork your self-doubt**. Experience is what gives you conviction to ask for someone's entire year's salary.

## Real numbers to cite
- Gym Launch entered at **$16,000** for a 16-week done-with-you intensive, when low-price competitors charged **$500/mo** and the top competitor charged **$5,000**. That is 3x the highest player and 32x the lowest. Then upsold 35% into a **$42,000/yr, three-year** agreement. (p51)
- Photography client: average ticket **$300 to $1,500** (5x), which **38x'd profit** ($1,000/wk to $38,000/wk). Fewer hours per customer and higher satisfaction. (p54)
- **"99 percent of businesses need to raise their prices to grow, not lower them. Profit is oxygen."** (p52)

## Applying this
1. Never discount the core offer. Add bonuses instead (see 07-bonuses).
2. Raise value first, then price. Never the reverse.
3. If you must justify the price, justify it against what the client makes or saves, not against competitors.
