# Attraction Offers

**The job:** turn strangers into customers, and get enough cash up front to cover the cost of getting them and delivering to them - more than once over.

> "The point of Attraction Offers is to turn strangers into customers. And, to do it in a way that gets more cash up front. Ideally, we get enough cash to cover the cost of the customer and the cost to deliver our thing multiple times over. That way we can pay ourselves back and get our next customer." (p65)

## The principle underneath all five
Strangers cannot judge your value. They can judge your price.

> "Strangers can only take your word on the value. But, they absolutely understand the price. For that reason, discounts make anything a great deal to just about anyone. And, the greater the discount, the better the deal. The greatest discount of all being free." (p19)

**Free and discount are the same lever at different settings** (figure p20). Anywhere he says "free" you can substitute "discount" or "$1," and the reverse. They sit on one continuum from 100% off (highest demand) to full price (lowest demand).

And the trick that makes free profitable:
> "But what's a better deal than free stuff? More and better free stuff. One free thing is awesome. Two free things are awesomer And, maybe to get those two free things, they have to buy one. That's how we make money on free stuff." (p20)

## Which of the five to pick

| Offer | Use it when | Page |
|---|---|---|
| **Win Your Money Back** | the customer has to keep showing up for the result. Fitness, coaching, learning a skill, anything people start and quit | p23 |
| **Giveaways** | you want a flood of qualified leads fast, and your thing has a big believable retail value | p31 |
| **Decoy Offer** | you know what gets results better than the customer does, and you want a premium option to be the obvious choice | p39 |
| **Buy X Get Y Free** | it makes sense to buy more of the thing, or to buy longer access to it | p47 |
| **Pay Less Now or Pay More Later** | you can prove a clear yes/no result inside a short window | p55 |

## 1. Win Your Money Back (p23-30, figure p23)
**Structure:** they pay up front. If they do X, within Y time, within Z rules, they get the money back - as cash or store credit.

Three ways to set the criteria (p24):
- **Results** - they bet on their own ability to hit the goal (make $X, lose Z lbs)
- **Actions** - they bet on their ability to follow directions (attend, log, post, do homework)
- **Actions and Results** - both. His preference for people who lack the skill to do it alone: "By setting a good goal for them and showing how they reach it, they have a fighting chance."

**Good criteria have three characteristics (p27):**
1. **Easy to track.** Bonus points if people already do it - phones already track steps, cameras date photos.
2. **Gets customers results.** "Whatever stuff the best customers do to get the best results, make everyone do it."
3. **Advertises the business.** Build it into the criteria: posting progress, tagging, referring, leaving reviews and testimonials.

**Hard numbers and rules:**
- "This offer has generated over $1 billion in sales industry-wide." (p26)
- From thousands of gyms, **about 10% of all customers will ask for their money back.** "If you can't stomach it, don't do this." (p26)
- **Only run this if your refund rate is already below 5%.** Otherwise fix the product first. (p29)
- Store credit converts the same as cash back in his testing, so use store credit. (p27)

**The part everyone gets wrong (p28, marked IMPORTANT in the book):** do not hand back the winnings as free months. Apply the credit *across a longer, more expensive offer*. A customer wins $600 of credit on a $200/mo product: do not give three free months, give a $50/mo discount for 12 months, so they pay $150/mo.
> "people fall off if they don't pay something. A discount over the long haul keeps them engaged over the long haul. So it's in the customer's best interest to keep some skin in the game." (p28)

**Where the money actually comes from (p29):**
> "Everyone thinks businesses make money on people who fail the program. No. The real money comes from people who succeed with it and you have something else to offer them."

So the offer only works if you have an upsell ready to absorb the winnings. That upsell is the **Rollover Upsell** ([02-upsells.md](02-upsells.md)).

**Make every meeting an offer slot.** Make check-ins part of the money-back criteria. His gyms ran three: nutrition orientation to a supplement offer, progress check-in to a membership offer, transformation feedback to the membership offer again plus a prepay discount. (p28)

**Make everyone a winner, in private (p28-29).** Halfway through, make the next offer as if they already won. And at the end, let the losers win too:
> "we don't get customers to make a sale, we make sales to get customers." (p28)

## 2. Giveaways (p31-38, figures p31, p36)
**Structure:** advertise a chance to win a big prize in exchange for contact details. Announce one grand prize winner publicly. Then privately offer **everyone else** the same thing at a discount.

The six steps (p33): pick a Grand Prize, pick your promotional offer, ask for contact information and eligibility, pick the qualifying actions, put it on a deadline, announce the winner and contact everyone else.

**The rules with numbers in them:**
- **Make the Grand Prize the thing you want everyone to buy**, with a stated monetary value so it anchors. (p33)
- **Run it for seven days**, or until leads exceed the number of people you can call in seven days, whichever comes first. (p34, p37)
- **The promotional offer discount = 10% to 30% of gross margins.** Worked example: $5,000 value, $2,000 retail, everyone else gets it at $1,800. "By comparing the value of the thing to what they pay, a 10% discount becomes a 64% difference in cost-to-value!" (p34)
- **Claiming expires in seven days.** Urgency in three places: to enter, to claim, to use. (p34, p37)
- **Two grand prizes doubles the leads.** Tell entrants that if someone they refer wins, they win the other prize. Referrers then police quality themselves. (p36, and the real Skool post at )
- **Have downsells ready** for the ones who won't take the promotional offer: same percentage discount on any other product that fits. (p37)

**The failure mode, stated as a rule (p36):**
> "If Your Giveaway Doesn't Work, It Means Your Grand Prize Wasn't Grand Enough."

His portfolio company gave away tickets to their own event and got nothing. They ran it again with a $50,000 equipment bundle plus a year of the core product and it crushed.

**Legal:** sweepstakes are heavily regulated and a badly run one is an illegal lottery. Someone must actually win. Rules and qualifications must be clear. Get counsel. (p31, p35)

## 3. Decoy Offer (p39-45, figures p39, p41)
**Structure:** advertise a lesser, cheaper, stripped version. When the lead engages, present it side by side with a much better premium version, and lean on the premium one.

> "By putting your decoy offers and premium offers side-by-side, leads can see how much more valuable your premium offer is." (p41)

Why he likes it: you close everyone either way. "If they take the premium, great. If they take the decoy, also great. It gives you time to upgrade them rather than losing them." (p41)

**How to build the decoy (p42):** fewer components, older models, less personalisation, **and remove any guarantees.** "Your Attraction Offer only has to get leads to engage. Nothing more."

**Make the contrast huge.** "So make the decoy option as basic as reasonable. Then make the premium option as awesome as possible. The bigger the contrast, the better the deal, and the more customers will take it." (p43)

**Free vs discount, the show-up tradeoff (p43):**
> "If you run a Free Attraction offer, you'll get more leads. If you run a Discount Offer, you'll get fewer leads, but a higher percentage will show up."

Use a discount version wherever a no-show is expensive - doctors, lawyers, dentists.

**Advertise benefits, not features.** "We advertise a transformation in 21 days, not workouts and meal plans. Leads get specific product details in the sales presentation, not in the advertising!" (p42)

**The permission question (p43)** - the single most useful line in the chapter:
> "Are you here for free stuff or lasting results?"

Most say results, and you skip straight to the premium offer. If they say free stuff, present the decoy, contrast it immediately, then ask "which do you think will get you to your goal faster?"

**Four ways to say the same discount (p43),** all worth testing: percentage off (25% off), absolute amount ($300 off), free portion (3 months free), total package (one year for $900, was $1,200).

His real result: two options, free one-session-a-week vs a $399 "Ultimate" version with a repeat-free guarantee. **Eight out of ten took the $399.** (p41)

## 4. Buy X Get Y Free (p47-53, figures p47, p48, p49, p50, p51)
**Structure:** they buy one thing, they get other things free. The reframe that makes a discount into a free offer.

The core arithmetic (p48-49):
- Three t-shirts at $10 each = $30. Or **one t-shirt for $30 and two free.** Same money, far better offer.
- With a real discount: three shirts at $6.67 = $20 (33% off). Or **one shirt for $20 and two free.** Same money again.

Boot Factory tripled the price of one pair and gave two away. "an expensive pair of boots with two free pairs gets Boot Factory more customers than selling one pair at a fair price." (p49)

**The rules:**
- **Raise prices before giving stuff away.** "permanently raise prices to accommodate the discount. Don't lie. Actually raise your prices." (p50)
- **More free than paid.** "Buy ten get two free is not as strong as buy two get ten free." Use "buy one get two," not "buy two get one." (p50)
- **The free things can differ from the paid things.** Buy a $10 shirt, get $20 of socks free. (p50)
- **More cheap free things can beat fewer expensive ones.** "Buy 1 Shirt Get 1 Shirt Free" vs "Buy 1 Shirt, Get 3 Socks Free" - test it. (p51)
- **The 18-month test (p49).** Good: buy 12 get 6 free. Better: buy 9 get 9 free. Best: buy 6 get 12 free. All $1,800, all 18 months of service. The third is the most compelling because it has the most free stuff.
- **It lengthens retention.** If normal customers stay three months, "Buy 2 Get 2 Free" keeps them four - four months of chances to make more offers. (p53)
- **Fast cash from existing customers:** make the offer to current recurring customers, but **cap it at 10% of your base** so recurring cash flow stays healthy. (p52)
- **Keep selling prepaid customers.** "these are people who spend the most money... Their wallets have been 'refreshed' with new money." (p52)

**The warning (p51):** this creates a huge cash pop for work you owe later.
> "Selling stuff you can't deliver on breaks the law and ruins your reputation. Deliver on your promises."

## 5. Pay Less Now or Pay More Later (p55-59, figure p55)
**Structure:** two options. Pay full price later (card on file, billed after, cancel if not satisfied) OR pay a discounted price now with bonuses.

> "This play works so well because we remove all risk from the customer. They pay later and only if they like it. So it combines the benefits of a delayed payment and a satisfaction guarantee." (p56)

The pay-later option lets you advertise "free" and **still gets the card on file**. Then you follow up with the pay-now offer.

**The numbers:**
- **Pay now = 20-50% discount plus bonuses.** (p56)
- **If more than 10% of pay-later people cancel the payment**, you promised too much, the guarantee conditions are too soft, or the price is too high. (p57)
- Balance the two: too many taking pay-later, sweeten pay-now. Too many taking pay-now, do the opposite. (p57)

**Two build rules (p57):**
1. **Promise a clear yes/no result you can deliver inside the window.** Rate the shoulder pain 1-10 before and after. "Keep the promise simple, clear, and measurable."
2. **Make it a conditional satisfaction guarantee.** They can only cancel the billing if they qualify - attended, showed up, turned in data. "After all, they can't say you suck if they never try it."

Works for recurring too: pay a higher ongoing rate 30 days later, or pay less today and keep the lower rate for good. (p58)

**The accidental lesson worth stealing (p58):** hint at your next offer early. He told a nutrition audience "everyone wants to know what supplements to buy. We'll get there promise. But please pay attention to the nutrition section - it matters more." He implied everyone bought supplements without offering them, and more people bought when he finally did.

## Free Goodwill Offer (p61-63, figure p61)
Not a sales offer. It is the chapter where he asks the reader to leave a review, using other readers' stories to do it. Worth knowing it exists and why it sits inside an Attraction Offers section: the ask is a free thing that costs the giver nothing and compounds his distribution. If the question is how to get reviews, the play is the same one: ask for the review by showing what it does for a stranger, and make it take under sixty seconds.

## What it built
> "They turned $1,000 into $10,000,000 in ten months because when I got returns I kept doubling down." (p65)
