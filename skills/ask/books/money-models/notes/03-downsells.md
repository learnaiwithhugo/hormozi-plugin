# Downsells

**The job:** turn a no into a yes without cutting your price for the same thing.

> "Downselling tweaks the original offer to find the highest value solution for the customer's budget. So any offer you make after someone says 'no' is a downsell." (p101)

Two levers, and only two:
- **Change how they pay** → [04-payment-plans-and-trials.md](04-payment-plans-and-trials.md)
- **Change what they get** → Feature Downsells, below

---

## The rules of downselling (p102-103)

**1. They said no to this offer, not all offers.**
> "No means no for this thing, not no for everything." (p102)

**2. Downsells are trades.**
> "When downselling, you work with the customer to find combinations of giving and getting until you get a match. If you're gonna give something, get something." (p102)

**3. Personalize, don't pressure.** Offer more of what they like and less of what they don't, with a price to match. "If I can better serve them, it would be offensive not to." (p103)

**4. Offer the same things in new ways.**
> "just think of downselling more like a hundred ways to offer the stuff you already have." (p103)

**5. Don't drop your price just to get somebody to buy.**
> "dropping your price is not really downselling, it's discounting." (p103)

**6. Customers talk about price.** Testing prices deliberately, to a set number of people, planned ahead, is fine. Cutting the price in the moment because you're scared of losing the sale is not. "If they find out someone got the same thing for less 'just because' - you'll upset people." (p103)

### The cautionary story (p102)
A car salesman dropped $5,000 insurance to $400 across one conversation. The buyer still said no.
> "At first I said no because it was too much money, but by the end I said no because I didn't trust the guy. The entire experience felt dirty. Then I wondered, was he ripping me off on the car too? Now, I didn't want to buy the car from him either!"

The fix, stated as the rule: **you can offer something different for less. You just can't offer the same thing for less.** (p102)

## Feature Downsells (p123-129, figure p123)

**The formula:** take something away, lower the price, and ask "how about now?" (p124)

> "Feature Downsells lower prices by changing what customers get. I do them by offering lesser quantity, lower quality, lower price alternatives, or cutting optional components." (p124)

### The mechanism that makes it work
Every feature has a price and a value, and removing it changes both.
- **Remove stuff they hate and lower the price a lot** → they get a better deal, more take the downsell
- **Remove stuff they love and lower the price a little** → they get a worse deal, more go back to the original offer

> "People will see the value in the thing you removed after they see the difference in price." (p124)

Which is why you **remove features from highest value to lowest.** That order gets customers to "re-upsell" themselves onto the more expensive offer. (p124)

### The story, with the numbers (p123-124)
A high-ticket seller stopped discounting and started removing his **full money-back guarantee** instead.
> "If you don't want the option to get your money back, you can pay less. Or, you can keep your money-back guarantee - which would you prefer?"

Before: 100 calls, 25 bought the one full-price option.
After: **35 bought the main thing and 40 took the downsell.** Close rate went from 25% to 75%, and full-price buyers went *up*.

**So: guarantees are features. Make removing yours part of your standard downsell process.** (p129)

### The feature menu to downsell from (p125-126)
**Quantity:** fewer sessions, shorter duration, fewer units. "Instead of a three-month supply, how about we start with just one?"
**Product quality:** older versions, lesser materials, lower social status materials. Leather to vinyl.
**Service quality** - his full list, all of which also work *upward* to raise price:
- Time availability (set times vs whenever), days of week, times of day, length of call
- Location availability (one location vs all)
- Cancellations (reschedule fees vs free)
- Speed of response (minutes vs hours vs days)
- Speed of delivery (wait in line vs priority, same day vs next week)
- Service ratio (one-on-one vs one-to-many vs many-to-one)
- Communication method (text vs chat vs video)
- Provider qualifications (owner vs long-time employee vs new employee)
- Live vs recorded
- In-person vs remote
- **DIY, DWY, DFY** - Do It Yourself vs Done With You vs Done For You
- Expirations (forever vs X time vs specific times)
- Personalization (generic vs made just for you)
- Insurance/guarantee: length, coverage, conditional vs unconditional

**Removing entire features:** drop the calls, keep chat and email. **DFY to DIY:** the chiropractor sells at-home tools, the painter leases you the spray machine, and Alex's own version - "Instead of me and my team buying your company and actively growing your business, why don't you just attend a workshop?" (p126)

### The operating rules
- **Never negotiate the price.** "People who demand to pay less for the same thing are business terrorists. I don't negotiate with terrorists." (p127) If they want to pay less *now*, offer a payment plan. If they want to pay less *overall*, offer a feature downsell. But nobody pays less just because.
- **Stay a helpful guide, not a salesman.** "If you act pushy, your offers will exhaust customers faster. If you stay a helpful guide, you can downsell as many offers as necessary without exhausting the customer." (p127)
- **Standardize the process once you know your customers.** "First, I cut something valuable and lower the price a little. I do this to get them to reconsider the original offer/price. If that fails, I continue removing features and lowering prices until they buy." (p127)
- **Name the combinations.** Most expensive gets an aspirational status name - "The Whale Package," "The Total Transformation," "High Roller." Cheapest is called **"The Minimum"** - "I like it because it implies they have to get at least that thing." Then: "so nothing more than the minimum package then?" to get a no that means yes. (p127)
- **Temperature check after two downsells.** "Got it. Real quick. I want to make sure. On a sale from 1-10 how bad do you want this?" 8 or above, switch to payment plan downselling. 7 or below, ask "What would a 10 look like?" and recombine features. (p127)
- **Alternate between feature and payment plan downsells.** "When you use both, you become very difficult to refuse." (p127)
- **After each downsell, ask "Deal?" or "Fair enough?"** "This works astonishingly well. Fewer people will see you change the offer for them and then say 'No that's not fair.'" (p128)

### Two extras worth stealing
**Free orientations boost DIY downsells (p128).** Once someone has refused every Done-For-You offer: "Even though we're not gonna work together on X, I still want to help. How about you just come to a free orientation on X tomorrow?" About half showed up, and almost all of them bought. "Free money for little extra work."

**Feature downsell current customers before they cancel (p129).** When a customer isn't using a feature, offer them a lower price for only what they use.
> "Customers who we've downsold into a lower package just for them have the second highest value of all my customers. When people have a product they like at a price they find fair, they keep paying."

**Barter with reviews, testimonials and referrals (p129).** Discount in exchange for advertising, not for nothing: "I'll knock $100 off if you: 1) Leave me a review on all review sites 2) Leave me a video testimonial 3) Make a public social post at the beginning, middle, and end of our program showing your progress 4) Introduce me to two friends who you would want to do this with. Deal?"

## The closing frame (p131, figure p131)
> "it's less about having a hundred different products with the same offer, and more about having a hundred different offers for the same product. But, no matter what, the offer is never the same stuff for cheaper."
