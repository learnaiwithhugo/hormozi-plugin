# Diagnostics

**Start here when the question is vague.** Work down. The first "no" is usually the answer.

---

## 0. Is this a Money Model problem at all?
The single question:

> "A good Money Model makes more profit from a customer than it costs to get and service them in the first 30 days. That's the bare minimum." (p169)

| What's true | Diagnosis |
|---|---|
| You make a profit per customer but it takes months to arrive | **Cash timing problem.** You have a business, not a Money Model. → section 2 |
| You make no profit per customer at all | **Offer problem.** Nothing here fixes negative unit economics. Raise the price or cut the cost first |
| You can't get customers at all | **Advertising problem, not a Money Model problem.** That's $100M Leads |
| You get customers profitably but slowly, and can't scale | **This book.** → section 3 |

## 1. "I only sell one thing"
This is the single most common finding in the book, and he says it bluntly:

> "You barely have a business - you have a front end. Figure out what you're gonna offer next." (p73)

Businesses that fix this report **5x'ing.** The burger math is the proof: $0.25 profit becomes $2.00 with fries and a drink, and $3.00 supersized - an **11.6x** increase, from offers that cost nothing new to build. (p68, figure p68)

**Action:** pick one upsell. → [02-upsells.md](02-upsells.md)

## 2. "I'm profitable but always broke"
You are on the slow drip. (p12)

Checks, in order:
1. **Do you make your money back inside 30 days?** If not, that is the whole problem. (p13)
2. **Are you billing monthly?** Switch to four-week cycles: 13 cycles a year instead of 12 is **8.3% more revenue for changing a few words**, and 41% more annual profit at 20% margins. (p149)
3. **Are you presenting cheapest-first?** Present most cash up front to least. It also lowers churn: monthly billing churns at **10.7%/month**, quarterly at 5%, annual at 2%. (p110)
4. **Are you charging a processing fee?** 3% straight to the bottom line, and nobody refuses over it. (p149)
5. **Do you have a bulk prepay upsell?** "buy five months get one free" - **one in eight takers raises 30-day profits by 50%.** (p143)

→ [06-cash-and-billing.md](06-cash-and-billing.md)

## 3. "Nobody is buying my front-end offer"
| Symptom | Likely cause | Fix |
|---|---|---|
| Not enough leads at all | the offer isn't a good enough deal | move down the free/discount continuum toward free (p19) |
| Giveaway gets no entries | **"your Grand Prize wasn't grand enough"** (p36) | give away something people actually want, or better for that audience |
| Leads come but don't show up | free attracts more leads and fewer show-ups | switch to a discount offer instead of free. Especially where a no-show is expensive (p43) |
| They engage but take the cheap version | the contrast isn't big enough | strip the decoy further, make the premium better, present premium first (p43) |
| The offer works but you lose money on it | you gave away value without raising price | "permanently raise prices to accommodate the discount. Don't lie. Actually raise your prices." (p50) |

→ [01-attraction-offers.md](01-attraction-offers.md)

## 4. "My upsells aren't landing"
His own list of why upsells fail (p69):
- **You offer something they don't want** - too different, or it doesn't solve their problem
- **You offer it at the wrong time** - before they've experienced the problem
- **You offer it the wrong way** - they don't believe you
- Or a combination

Then check:
- **Are you offering the higher-profit thing first?** (p73)
- **Is the upsell integrated into the first purchase**, so the problem surfaces naturally? (p74)
- **Can they have it immediately?** "A $10,000 thing you get later is worth less than a $10,000 thing you get now." (p74)
- **Do you have a premium offer at all?** "The Only Thing Worse Than Making A $1,000 Offer To A Person With A $100 Budget...is Making A $100 Offer To Someone With A $1,000 Budget." You lose $900 in the second case. (p89)
- **Are you booking the next meeting from this one?** No BAMFAM, no second chance to offer. (p74)

→ [02-upsells.md](02-upsells.md)

## 5. "People say no and I don't know what to do"
You have exactly two moves, and the order matters (p101):
1. **Change how they pay** - price stays the same → [04-payment-plans-and-trials.md](04-payment-plans-and-trials.md)
2. **Change what they get** - price comes down → [03-downsells.md](03-downsells.md)

**Never the third thing.** "You can offer something different for less. You just can't offer the same thing for less." (p102) Dropping the price for the same thing costs you the sale *and* the trust - see the car insurance story (p102).

**Alternate between the two.** "When you use both, you become very difficult to refuse." (p127)

## 6. "My payment plans are hurting me"
The test (p110): after adding payment plans, **close rate should rise while the number of paid-in-fulls stays the same.**

> "if the number of paid-in-fulls goes down, you have a problem. You've just put people who would've paid in full on payment plans!"

Also check:
- **Do you reward paying in full instead of punishing paying late?** Price with interest included, then discount for prepayment. (p107)
- **Are payments aligned to paycheck dates?** And do you re-run declines the same day? That recovers about a third. (p109-110)
- **Are you re-offering the paid-in-full discount mid-plan?** Customers forget it exists. (p109)

## 7. "My churn is killing me"
In order of leverage:
1. **Billing cadence.** Monthly 10.7%, quarterly 5%, annual 2%. (p110)
2. **Does leaving cost more than staying?** Waived Fee Offer, fee at 3-5x monthly, minimum one year. (p154-156)
3. **Did you hand back credit as free months?** Never do that - people fall off if they do not have to pay anything. "A discount over the long haul keeps them engaged over the long haul." Spread it as a monthly discount instead. (p28)
4. **Is there a lifetime discount sitting just past your average drop-off month?** (p150)
5. **Can they find the cancel button?** If not they vanish and complain publicly instead of complaining to you. (p150)
6. **Are you exit-interviewing?** "I routinely save a third of customers that agree to exit interviews." (p151)
7. **Are you feature-downselling before they cancel?** Those customers end up the second most valuable he has. (p129)

**But check the product first.** "Pricing incentivizes sticking but it can't (and shouldn't) overcome a terrible product. You want to nudge them, not handcuff people into paying for something they hate." (p155)

## 8. The number thresholds, in one table
| Metric | Threshold | Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Refund rate before running Win Your Money Back | must be **under 5%** | otherwise fix the product first (p29) |
| Win Your Money Back claim rate | **~10%** of customers | normal, budget for it (p26) |
| Pay Later cancellations | over **10%** | over-promised, guarantee too soft, or price too high (p57) |
| Waived Fee early cancels | over **5%** | product problem, not a pricing problem (p155) |
| Anchor premium price | **5-10x** the main offer | below that it doesn't anchor (p91) |
| Rollover next-offer price | at least **4x** the credit | keeps the discount at 25% or less (p97) |
| Gift card value | **20%** of the next offer's price | $200 card against a $1,000+ offer (p97) |
| Waived fee size | **3-5x** monthly rate | smaller (1.5-3x) if you need up-front cash instead (p155) |
| Standalone vs continuity price | **1.33x** for a 50/50 split, up to **2.66x** for 90% continuity | (p142) |
| Giveaway promotional discount | **10-30%** of gross margins | (p34) |
| Giveaway run length | **7 days**, or your call capacity | whichever comes first (p37) |
| Buy X Get Y Free to existing customers | cap at **10%** of your base | protects recurring cash flow (p52) |

## 9. "I'm trying to build the whole thing at once"
The most likely cause of everything breaking.

> "Do not try and implement a full Money Model at once. It will break your business." (p167)

> "you'll need to measure in quarters, not weeks. You either build it right or you build it again." (p166)

Build order: get customers reliably → make them pay for themselves → make them pay for the next customer → maximise long-term value → spend everything you can on advertising. (p161)

And expect this:
> "when your Money Model starts working, your business starts breaking. Part of the game." (p161)

→ [08-build-your-money-model.md](08-build-your-money-model.md)

## 10. "I need more things to sell"
Almost always the wrong conclusion.

> "Simple Scales. Fancy Fails... it's less about having 100 products to offer, and more about having 100 ways to offer your product. Think more ways to sell the same thing, not more things to sell." (p166)

And if you genuinely have a gap: **sell somebody else's stuff for a commission.** "you can always offer somebody else's stuff in your Money Model." (p166)
