# Churn and cancellations

## Have the cancellation policy decided before you sell anything
> "You Need To Have A Cancellation Policy Figured Out Ahead Of Time. There are many common ones. 30 or 60 days notice. Cancellation fees. Cancel anytime." (p150)

**His favourite, because everyone joins on a discount:** make the cancellation fee equal to the discount they agreed to take.
> "So if they got $600 in discounts by committing, they can pay $600 whenever they want to cancel. This is simple to explain." (p150)

That puts them back at the month-to-month rate, which is fair and easy to say out loud.

**The tradeoff, stated plainly (p151):** "Light cancellation terms get more people to sign up but more people leave. Harsher terms get fewer sign-ups but fewer leave."

## Make it easy to cancel
> "If customers have nowhere to complain inside your business, they will definitely complain outside of your business. If you have no obvious way for them to cancel, more people will vanish and complain. By having a clear way for them to contact you, then you can have a real chance to save it. Small businesses don't get rich by making stuff hard for their customers. If you make it easy, you'll suffer fewer 1-star reviews and have a chance to save them when they do - because you'll know about it." (p150)

## The exit interview
> "If A Customer Wants To Cancel, Ask To Do An Exit Interview. Some people like to vent. Let them. Get more angry about the problem than them. They may try to calm you down. Sometimes, they will save themselves!" (p150)

**Incentivise it with the fee you already have:** "I'll waive your cancellation fee if you come in and tell me what I could do better." (p150-151)

**The number:** "I routinely save a third of customers that agree to exit interviews." (p151)

**And the counterintuitive save (p150):** people cancelling a cheap package often wanted the expensive one.
> "I've had many people buy a lower cost offer and then complain because they wanted the higher cost features. So I offer higher cost features, and they buy."

That is a **Rollover Upsell into a higher level of service**, not a discount. See [02-upsells.md](02-upsells.md).

## Downsell them before they cancel
> "Feature Downsell Current Customers. Customers who use all the features they pay for keep paying longer than people who don't. So once you see a customer isn't using a feature, offer a lower price - only paying for the features they use." (p129)

Either they say they want to keep it and start using it again, or they're grateful for a better deal. "It takes work, but it beats them canceling."

**The finding worth remembering:**
> "Customers who we've downsold into a lower package just for them have the second highest value of all my customers. When people have a product they like at a price they find fair, they keep paying." (p129)

## Structural churn levers, ranked by leverage
1. **Billing cadence.** Monthly billing churns at 10.7%/month, quarterly at 5%, annual at 2%, across 14,000 businesses. Start high and work down. (p110)
2. **Waived fees.** "Customers will stay longer if leaving costs more than staying." (p154) Fee at 3-5x monthly, commitment at a year minimum. See [05-continuity.md](05-continuity.md).
3. **Lifetime discount at your churn point.** Find the month your average customer drops off, and put an earned lifetime lower rate just past it. Tell them up front, and remind them as it approaches. (p150)
4. **Backloaded discounts.** "Backloading discounts converts fewer customers, but they lower churn." (p151)
5. **Skin in the game.** Never hand winnings or credit back as free months. "people fall off if they don't pay something. A discount over the long haul keeps them engaged over the long haul." (p28)

## Winbacks - the customers you already lost
> "Previous Customers Are Still Customers. Upsell Them. Reach out to old customers (6+ months since last purchase). Look at how much they paid before. Decide how much you're willing to roll over. Offer it." (p96)

His own run: personalised videos for **200 past customers**, offering **$4,000 of credit** to return. **About 20% took it.** One day of recording produced roughly **$1,900,000 of annual revenue.**

## The refund rule that sits above all of this
> "If A Customer Asks For Their Money Back - Give It Back. Avoid the headache. And if you made a goof - fix the goof. Don't be a silly goose. Treat customers well. Next time, spend the time and resources to get better customers." (p17)

> "Don't Take Blood Money. If someone doesn't want me to have their money, I want it less than they do. As a personal rule, if a customer asks for a refund - entitled or not - I give it to them. Just focus on getting the next customer." (p27)

**But do the rollover before the refund.** "This has saved me tons of customers and cash. If you did a bad job (hey, it happens), roll over for a 'do over.' And if they want something different, roll over their purchase toward that thing instead." (p96)

## The thresholds that mean something is wrong
| Signal | Threshold | Read it as |
|---|---|---|
| Win Your Money Back refunds | above 5% refund rate before you launch it | fix the product first, don't run the offer (p29) |
| Win Your Money Back claims | about 10% will ask for their money back | normal, budget for it (p26) |
| Pay Later cancellations | more than 10% cancel the charge | you promised too much, the guarantee conditions are too soft, or the price is too high (p57) |
| Waived Fee early cancels | more than 5% want out early | the product is the problem, not the pricing (p155) |
